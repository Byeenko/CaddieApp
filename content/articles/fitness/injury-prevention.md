> [!key] Key points
> - **Lower back** problems are the most common golf injury, often from limited hip or upper-back mobility.
> - **Elbows and wrists** suffer from hitting fat shots, thick rough and mats, and from too many balls.
> - **Warm up**, build practice volume **gradually**, and **stop when it hurts**.
> - See a **physiotherapist** for pain that lasts or keeps coming back.

> [!note] Not medical advice
> This lesson gives general information. If you have pain or an injury, get it assessed by a physiotherapist, sports doctor or GP.

## The common problem areas

| Area | Common causes | Prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Lower back** | Limited hip/upper-back rotation; lots of side bend; poor posture; cold starts | Mobility work, core strength, warm-ups (see [[mobility-for-golf]]) |
| **Elbow** (inner: "golfer's elbow"; outer: "tennis elbow") | Hitting the ground hard, heavy rough, big practice sessions | Build volume slowly; forearm strength; good strike |
| **Wrist** (often lead wrist) | Fat shots, digging into hard ground, rough | Avoid excessive digging; tape on hard mats; rest |
| **Shoulder** | Over-swinging; lack of mobility; poor posture | Shoulder mobility and strength; controlled backswing |

## Habits that protect you
- **Warm up every time:** 5 minutes of dynamic movement before hitting (see [[warm-up-and-pre-round]]).
- **Build up gradually.** Going from no practice to 200 balls in a day is a classic injury trigger.
- **Hit off grass when you can.** Firm mats send more shock through the wrists and elbows on fat shots.
- **Stay hydrated and sun-safe** on hot days. Fatigue leads to poor movement.
- **Strength train** the core, hips and forearms (see [[strength-and-speed]]).
- **Use a push or pull buggy**, or a cart, if carrying the bag aggravates your back.

## When to stop
- **Sharp pain** during a swing: stop for the day.
- **Pain that lingers** for more than a few days, or keeps coming back: get it assessed.
- **Pain, tingling or numbness** that travels down a leg or arm: see a doctor promptly.

> [!mistake] Playing through pain
> Changing your swing to avoid pain usually creates a new problem elsewhere. Getting an injury checked early is the fastest way back to full-speed golf.

> [!tip] Recovery after golf
> A short walk and some gentle held stretches after a round help you feel better the next day, especially for the hips and lower back.
