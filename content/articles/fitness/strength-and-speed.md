> [!key] Key points
> - **Speed training** (short sets of maximum-effort swings) is one of the most reliable ways to add club speed.
> - **Strength**, especially in the legs, hips and core, gives you a base to create and control that speed.
> - **Rest matters.** Speed and power improve with quality, not volume.
> - **Mobility comes first.** Speed through a restricted range of motion strains the body.

> [!note] Health first
> General information, not personal advice. If you have back, shoulder or joint issues, check with a physiotherapist before starting speed or strength training.

## Speed training
The principle: your body learns to move faster by **practising moving fast**. Programmes usually involve:
- **Overspeed swings** with a lighter-than-normal club or stick (faster than you normally swing)
- **Normal-weight** swings at maximum effort
- **Heavier** swings for strength in the motion
- Swinging **both ways** (right- and left-handed) for balance

**Typical structure:** 3 sessions a week, 10–15 minutes each, for 6–8 weeks, with a radar or launch monitor to track speed. Commercial systems (weighted-stick sets) come with structured programmes.

> [!warning] Warm up first
> Maximum-effort swings on a cold body are a common way to strain the back or obliques. Always do your mobility routine first.

## Strength basics
You don't need a bodybuilder's programme. Two sessions a week covering these patterns helps:

| Pattern | Example exercises | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Legs** (push) | Squats, split squats, step-ups | Pushing against the ground |
| **Hips** (hinge) | Romanian deadlifts, hip thrusts | Powerful hip extension |
| **Core** (resist and rotate) | Pallof press, side planks, cable rotations | Transferring force; protecting the back |
| **Upper body** (pull and push) | Rows, push-ups | Posture and shoulder health |
| **Power** | Medicine-ball rotational throws, jumps | Speed of movement |

## Putting it together
1. **Mobility** most days (see [[mobility-for-golf]]).
2. **Strength** twice a week.
3. **Speed** sessions three times a week, kept short and sharp.
4. **Test** your club speed every two weeks.
5. **Take it to the range**: once speed goes up, spend time making sure you can still hit the centre of the face (see [[driving-for-distance]]).

> [!mistake] Adding speed, losing strike
> A few extra mph means nothing if the strike falls apart. Combine speed work with practice at your new speed until the smash factor stays high.

> [!tip] Track it
> Note your top speed with the driver at the start of a programme and every two weeks. Seeing the numbers climb is the best motivation there is.
