> [!key] Key points
> - The swing needs **rotation**: mainly from the **hips** and the **upper back** (thoracic spine).
> - If those are stiff, the **lower back** and **arms** take over, costing speed and increasing injury risk.
> - A few minutes of **mobility work** most days makes a real difference.
> - Before golf, do **dynamic** movements; save long, held stretches for after.

> [!note] Health first
> This is general information, not personal medical advice. If you have pain, an injury or a medical condition, see a physiotherapist or doctor before starting new exercises.

## Where the swing needs movement

| Area | Why it matters | Stiff here often leads to… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Hips** (rotation) | Lets the pelvis turn back and through | Swaying, a restricted turn, lower-back strain |
| **Upper back** (thoracic rotation) | Creates a full shoulder turn | Lifting the arms instead of turning; over-the-top moves |
| **Shoulders** | Room for the arms at the top and finish | Short backswing, chicken wing |
| **Ankles** | Stable base, pressure shift | Poor balance and weight transfer |

## Quick self-checks
- **Seated rotation:** sit on a chair, hold a club across your chest, and turn each way without your hips moving. Can you turn about 45° each way?
- **Hip rotation:** lying on your back with one knee bent to 90°, let the knee fall in and out. Compare sides. Big differences are common and worth working on.

## A 10-minute mobility routine
*Example routine. Move slowly and stay comfortable; nothing should hurt.*

| Exercise | Reps | For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cat–cow (on hands and knees) | 8 slow reps | Spine |
| Open book (lying on your side, rotate the top arm open) | 8 each side | Upper-back rotation |
| 90/90 hip switches (sitting, rotate knees side to side) | 8 each side | Hip rotation |
| World's greatest stretch (lunge, elbow to instep, rotate up) | 5 each side | Hips, upper back, ankles |
| Arm circles and doorway chest stretch | 30 seconds | Shoulders |

## Before a round
Use **dynamic** movements, not long holds: leg swings, trunk rotations with a club across your shoulders, arm circles, walking lunges. See [[warm-up-and-pre-round]].

> [!mistake] Forcing a bigger turn
> If you lack mobility, cranking the backswing further usually comes from the lower back or a collapsed lead arm. Improve mobility first; the turn will grow on its own.

> [!tip] Little and often
> Ten minutes most days beats an hour once a week. Doing your routine while watching TV is a perfectly good plan.
