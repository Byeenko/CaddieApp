> [!key] Key points
> - Strokes gained measures **each shot** against how many shots a benchmark golfer would need from the same spot.
> - It splits your game into **off the tee, approach, around the green and putting**.
> - It shows **where you really lose shots**, which is often not where you think.
> - Shot-tracking apps can calculate it for you.

## The idea
Developed by Columbia professor **Mark Broadie** and explained in his book *Every Shot Counts*, strokes gained uses a simple principle: from every spot on the course, a benchmark golfer takes an average number of shots to hole out.

**Example (using approximate tour benchmarks):**
- From 150 yards in the fairway, the average is about **2.9** shots.
- You hit it to 6 metres. From there the average is about **1.9** shots.
- Your shot "used" one stroke and moved you from 2.9 to 1.9, a gain of 1.0. So you gained **0.0** strokes: exactly average.
- If you'd hit it to 1 metre (average about 1.05), you'd have gained about **0.85** strokes.

Formula: **strokes gained = (expected before) − (expected after) − 1**.

## The four categories

| Category | Includes |
| --- | --- |
| **Off the tee** | Tee shots on par 4s and 5s |
| **Approach** | Shots to the green from outside about 30 yards (incl. par 3 tee shots) |
| **Around the green** | Chips, pitches, bunker shots inside about 30 yards |
| **Putting** | All putts |

## What it usually reveals
Broadie's research found that the **long game** (driving and approach play) explains most of the difference between better and worse golfers. That surprises many golfers, who assume putting is the main difference. Putting still matters, but a three-putt costs no more than a tee shot out of bounds.

For your own game, the answer is personal. That's the point: strokes gained tells **you** where to practise.

## Using it without data
If you don't track every shot, approximate it:
- **Off the tee:** count penalty strokes and shots where you couldn't go for the green.
- **Approach:** GIR and average distance from the hole.
- **Short game:** up-and-down percentage.
- **Putting:** three-putts and one-putts.

See [[reviewing-your-round]] for a simple tracking sheet.

> [!tip] Compare with your handicap level
> Many apps let you compare against a scratch golfer or your target handicap rather than tour players. It's more useful for setting realistic goals.

> [!mistake] Practising only what you enjoy
> Most golfers prefer hitting drivers or putting. Strokes gained might show your biggest loss is approach play from 100–150 yards. Practise what the numbers say.
