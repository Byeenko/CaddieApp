> [!key] Key points
> - The ball tends to go **right** (fade or slice).
> - **More knee flex**, weight back towards your **heels**.
> - **Aim left** of the target.
> - **Stay down** through the shot and take a little **more club**.

## Why it goes right
With the ball below your feet, the toe of the club sits down, so the face points **right** of the leading edge. You also have to stay bent over, which makes it harder to rotate and release, so the face tends to stay open.

## Setup
1. **Use the full length** of the club. Hold it near the end.
2. **Bend your knees more** and get **lower** to the ball, keeping your back fairly straight.
3. **Weight towards your heels** so the slope doesn't tip you towards the ball.
4. **Ball in the centre** of your stance.
5. **Aim left.** The steeper the slope, the more.
6. **Take one more club.** The restricted swing loses distance.

## The swing
- A **three-quarter swing** is plenty. Balance is everything.
- **Stay down.** The most common error is standing up during the swing, which brings the club up off the ground and tops or thins the ball.
- Keep your **lower body quiet**, like a pitch shot with a longer swing.

> [!mistake] Standing up through impact
> If your knees straighten during the swing, you'll catch the ball thin or miss it entirely. Feel like you keep your knee flex until after the ball has gone.

> [!tip] Fairway woods from this lie
> Avoid them. A long club with the ball below your feet is very hard to control. A mid-iron or hybrid back to the fairway is usually the smarter play.

> [!drill] Knee-flex check
> On a below-the-feet slope, hold your finish and check your knees. If they've straightened, repeat with the feeling of sitting down a little more through impact. Five good, balanced swings and you'll trust it.
