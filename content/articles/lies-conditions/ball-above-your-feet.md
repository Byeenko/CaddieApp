> [!key] Key points
> - The ball tends to go **left** (draw or hook).
> - **Stand taller** and **grip down** the club.
> - **Aim right** of the target. The steeper the slope, the more.
> - Expect a **flatter, more rounded** swing.

## Why it goes left
When the ball is above your feet, the clubface naturally points **left** of where the leading edge aims, because the club sits on a slope with the toe up. Your swing also gets flatter, around your body, which encourages a draw. On a steep slope with a lofted club, the effect is bigger.

## Setup
1. **Grip down** the club, by as much as the ball is higher. The ball is closer to your hands.
2. **Stand a little taller**, with less bend from the hips.
3. **Weight slightly towards the balls of your feet**, so the slope doesn't pull you backwards.
4. **Ball in the middle** of your stance.
5. **Aim right.** A gentle slope might mean a couple of metres; a steep one with a wedge could be much more.

## The swing
- Make a **controlled, three-quarter** swing that feels **flatter**, more around your body.
- **Stay balanced.** If you lose your footing, you swung too hard.
- Expect slightly **less distance** because you've gripped down. Take a little more club if needed.

> [!mistake] Aiming at the flag
> If you aim straight at the target from this lie, the ball will finish left of it. Trust the setup and aim right.

> [!tip] Short game
> Chips with the ball above your feet also pull left. Aim right and grip down to the metal if you need to. Lower-lofted clubs are less affected, so a bump-and-run is often the easier option.

> [!drill] Sidehill test
> Find a sidehill slope with the ball above your feet. Hit five 7-irons aimed straight, then five aimed right with the setup above. See how much aim you actually need. Every slope and golfer is different.
