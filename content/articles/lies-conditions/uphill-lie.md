> [!key] Key points
> - The slope **adds loft**, so the ball flies **higher and shorter**. Take more club.
> - The ball tends to go **left**, so aim a little right.
> - Tilt your shoulders to **match the slope** and play the ball slightly **forward**.
> - Make a controlled swing and **swing up the hill** through impact.

## What the slope does

On an uphill lie your lead (left) foot is higher than your trail (right) foot. Three things happen:

1. **More loft.** Your body tilts with the slope, which adds loft to the club. A 7-iron can launch more like an 8 or 9-iron.
2. **Shorter carry.** Higher launch means less distance. The ball also lands more steeply, so it stops quickly with little roll.
3. **A pull or draw.** Your weight hangs back on your right side and your body struggles to rotate through, so the hands tend to flip and close the face. The ball goes left.

## Setup

- **Match the slope.** Tilt your shoulders so they run parallel to the ground, with the left shoulder higher. This naturally puts more weight on your right foot. Don't fight it.
- **Ball slightly forward** of where you'd normally play it, because the bottom of your swing moves up the slope.
- **Club up.** Use the table below as a starting point.
- **Aim right** of your target to allow for the pull. The steeper the slope, the more you'll need.
- **Grip down** slightly for control.

| Slope | Club adjustment | Aim |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gentle | One more club | A touch right |
| Moderate | One to two more clubs | Noticeably right |
| Steep | Two to three more clubs, shorter swing | Well right; prioritise balance |

These are starting points. Every golfer's numbers are different, so note how your own shots react.

## The swing

- Make a **three-quarter swing**. Balance matters more than power on a slope.
- **Swing along the slope.** Let the club follow the ground up the hill after impact, rather than trying to hit down into it.
- **Hold your finish.** If you can't hold your balance at the end, you swung too hard for the lie.

> [!mistake] Leaning into the hill
> Leaning your body into the slope feels natural, but it makes you swing down into the ground. The club digs in and the shot comes out fat and short. Tilt *with* the slope, not against it.

> [!tip] Around the green
> Uphill chips and pitches pop up and stop quickly. Play them a bit firmer than flat-ground shots of the same length, or use a lower-lofted club so the ball runs out more.

## In the caddie

When the GPS caddie arrives, slope will be one of the lie options you can tap. It will remind you to club up and aim right.
