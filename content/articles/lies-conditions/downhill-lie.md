> [!key] Key points
> - The slope **takes loft off**, so the ball flies **lower and runs further**.
> - It tends to go **right**, so aim a little left.
> - Tilt your shoulders to **match the slope** and play the ball a touch **back**.
> - **Swing down the slope** and chase the ball after impact.

## What the slope does
On a downhill lie your lead (left) foot is **lower** than your trail foot.

1. **Less loft.** Leaning with the slope de-lofts the club. A 7-iron flies more like a 6 or 5.
2. **More roll.** Lower flight plus a shallow landing means the ball runs.
3. **A push or fade.** It's hard to rotate and release fully going downhill, so the face tends to stay open and the ball goes right.
4. **Fat shots.** The ground behind the ball is higher, so it's easy to hit it first.

## Setup
- **Shoulders parallel to the slope:** lead shoulder lower. Your weight naturally moves onto your lead foot. Let it.
- **Ball a touch back** of normal, closer to your higher (trail) foot, so you catch it before the ground.
- **Club choice:** often the same club or one less, because of the lower flight and extra roll. On a steep slope, take more loft to help get it airborne.
- **Aim left** of your target to allow for the fade.

## The swing
- Make a **controlled, three-quarter** swing. Balance first.
- **Swing down the slope.** After impact, let the club and your body chase the ball down the hill. Feel like you're "walking after it".
- Don't try to lift the ball. Trust the loft.

> [!mistake] Hanging back
> Leaning into the hill (towards your trail foot) feels safe but puts the low point well behind the ball. The result is a fat shot or a topped one. Tilt with the slope.

> [!tip] Short game going downhill
> Downhill chips come out low and run. Use more loft (a sand or lob wedge), land the ball as close as possible, and let the slope do the rest.

> [!drill] Practice on a slope
> Find a gentle downhill slope near the practice area. Hit ten balls focusing only on matching your shoulders to the slope and chasing the ball after impact. Note which way they curve and by how much, so you know how much to aim off on the course.
