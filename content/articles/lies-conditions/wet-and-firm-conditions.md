> [!key] Key points
> - **Wet:** less roll, so the course plays **longer**. Greens are receptive: **fly it to the hole**.
> - **Firm:** more roll, so it plays **shorter**. Land it **short** and let it run.
> - **Mud on the ball** makes the flight unpredictable. Give yourself extra margin.
> - Adjust your **short game** to how the ground is reacting that day.

## Wet conditions
**How the course plays:**
- **Less roll** on drives and approaches, so take more club.
- **Soft greens** hold shots. You can fly it right to the flag.
- **Heavy, wet sand** in bunkers. The club doesn't bounce as much.
- **Mud balls:** mud on the ball can make it curve unpredictably, often away from the side with mud on it.

**Adjustments:**
- **Club up** on approaches into soft greens.
- Strike **ball first** and clean. Fat shots are worse on soggy ground.
- In wet bunkers, **open the face less** and take a little less sand.
- Keep **grips and gloves dry**: an umbrella over the bag, a towel under its hood, spare gloves.
- **Preferred lies:** in very wet conditions your club may allow placing the ball within a set distance on the fairway. Check the local rule on the day.

## Firm conditions
Common in Perth and other dry climates in summer.

**How the course plays:**
- **More roll**, so the course plays **shorter**, but trouble that's normally out of reach can come into play.
- **Fast greens** that don't hold high, spinning shots well.
- **Hard ground** that makes high-bounce wedges skip.

**Adjustments:**
- **Land approach shots short** of the target and let them run up, especially downwind.
- Off the tee, **check where your roll will finish**. A drive that normally stops before a bunker might run in.
- Around the green, the **bump-and-run** becomes your best friend (see [[flop-and-specialty-shots]]).
- Use **lower-bounce** wedges and pick the ball clean.
- **Putt from off the green** more often.

> [!mistake] Playing every day the same way
> The same 7-iron can stop dead in winter and bounce over the green in summer. In your warm-up, roll a few chips and putts to see how the ground is reacting, and adjust your landing spots.

> [!tip] Note it down
> In your round review, note the conditions alongside your score (see [[reviewing-your-round]]). Over a year you'll learn how your course changes with the seasons.
