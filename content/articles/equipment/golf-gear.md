> [!key] Key points
> - **Distance devices** (rangefinders, GPS) are allowed in most rounds, but **slope and wind** features must be **off** in competition.
> - **Shoes with good grip** help balance and power.
> - A **glove** on your lead hand helps grip, especially in heat.
> - Choose **training aids** that give clear feedback on one specific thing.

## Distance devices
- **Rangefinders** (laser) give the exact distance to the flag or any target. Very accurate.
- **GPS watches and apps** give front, middle and back of the green and hazard distances without needing a line of sight.

**The rules:** under the Rules of Golf you can use a device that measures **distance and direction**, unless a local rule bans them. Features that measure **slope (elevation), wind or temperature**, or give club recommendations, **can't be used in competition**. Most rangefinders have a slope switch for this reason. Check your club's competition conditions.

> [!note] Caddie and the rules
> The GPS caddie in this app (coming in Phase 4) will be **distance-only** in its first version, which is legal in most competitions. Advice features will sit behind a comp-mode switch.

## Shoes
- **Grip:** spikeless shoes suit dry conditions and double as everyday shoes; spiked shoes give more grip on wet or hilly courses.
- **Stability:** a firm, wide base helps you push against the ground.
- **Comfort:** you'll walk 8–10 km in a round.

## Gloves
- Worn on the **lead hand** (left hand for right-handers).
- Helps grip pressure stay light, especially in heat or humidity.
- Swap it out when it gets slick or worn. Many golfers keep two and rotate them on hot days.

## Training aids that earn their place

| Aid | What it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Alignment sticks | Aim, ball position, swing path. The most useful aid there is |
| Impact spray or tape | Showing where you strike the clubface |
| Putting mirror or gate | Eye position, aim and start line |
| Speed-training sticks | Building club speed (see [[strength-and-speed]]) |
| Launch monitor / simulator | Distances, face/path and spin numbers |

> [!mistake] Gadget overload
> Buying every aid at once leads to working on ten things and improving none. Pick one goal, then the one aid that gives clear feedback on it.

> [!tip] Australian summer
> Sunscreen, a hat and water are equipment too. On hot days, drink before you feel thirsty. Fatigue and dehydration ruin the back nine.
