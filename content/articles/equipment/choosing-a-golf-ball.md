> [!key] Key points
> - The biggest difference between balls is **greenside spin and feel**, not distance.
> - **Urethane-cover** balls spin more on short shots; **ionomer (Surlyn)-cover** balls are more durable and cheaper.
> - **Pick one model and stick with it**, so every chip and putt behaves the same.
> - Lose a lot of balls? Choose a cheaper one until that changes.

## How balls differ

| | Two-piece, ionomer cover | Multi-layer, urethane cover |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Feel | Firmer | Softer |
| Short-game spin | Lower: chips release more | Higher: chips check up |
| Driver spin | Lower, often straighter | Slightly higher |
| Durability | Very durable | Scuffs more easily |
| Price | Lower | Higher (tour balls) |

## Compression
Compression describes how much the ball squashes at impact. **Low-compression** balls feel soft and can suit slower swing speeds; **high-compression** balls suit faster swingers. In practice, feel and greenside spin are the more noticeable differences for most club golfers.

## How to choose
1. **Start around the green.** Hit chips and short pitches with a few different balls. Choose the one whose spin and feel you like.
2. **Then check the long game.** Make sure it doesn't balloon or spin too much with your driver.
3. **Think about cost.** If you lose a few balls a round, a mid-priced ball you're happy to lose is a sensible choice.
4. **Commit to one model.** Different balls react differently on chips and putts. Using the same ball every round makes your short game more consistent.

> [!mistake] Using whatever's in the bag
> Playing a tour ball on one hole, a found range ball on the next and a distance ball after that means every chip reacts differently. Consistency beats the "best" ball.

> [!tip] Mark it
> Put a unique mark on every ball with a pen. The rules require you to identify your ball, and it saves arguments when two people play the same model.

> [!drill] Five-ball test
> Take five different ball models to the short-game area. From the same spot, chip each ball to the same hole five times. Note which releases predictably and finishes closest. Then do the same with putting.
