> [!key] Key points
> - **Flex** should suit your **swing speed** and tempo.
> - **Lie angle** affects direction: a club that's too upright pulls shots left, too flat pushes them right.
> - **Length and grip size** affect posture and control.
> - A good **fitting** on a launch monitor is worth it once your swing is reasonably consistent.

## Shaft flex
Flex is how much the shaft bends. Too stiff tends to launch low and right; too soft tends to launch high, left and inconsistent.

| Driver swing speed | Typical flex |
| --- | --- |
| Under about 75 mph | Ladies or senior (A) |
| About 75–90 mph | Regular |
| About 90–105 mph | Stiff |
| Over about 105 mph | Extra stiff |

These are starting points. A smooth tempo might suit a softer flex at the same speed, and an aggressive transition a stiffer one.

## Shaft weight
- **Lighter shafts** can add a little speed, and suit slower swingers.
- **Heavier shafts** give more control and stability for faster or more aggressive swings.

## Lie angle
Lie angle is the angle between the shaft and the sole.
- **Too upright:** the heel digs in, the face points left, and shots go **left**.
- **Too flat:** the toe digs in, the face points right, and shots go **right**.

A fitter checks this by having you hit off a lie board, or with tape on the sole. Most irons can be bent a few degrees to fit.

## Length and grips
- **Length:** too long and you stand too upright and struggle to hit the centre; too short and you hunch over.
- **Grip size:** too small can encourage overactive hands (hooks); too big can restrict the release (slices).

## Is a fitting worth it?

| Situation | Recommendation |
| --- | --- |
| Complete beginner | Not yet. Get lessons and a basic set first |
| Consistent contact, buying new clubs | **Yes.** A fitting stops you buying the wrong specs |
| Specific issue (very low ball flight, everything left) | Yes. A lie or shaft change might be the fix |

> [!tip] Bring your own data
> Before a fitting, note your typical miss, your distances and what you want to improve. A good fitter will test against your current clubs, not just sell the newest model.

> [!mistake] Choosing flex by ego
> Playing a stiff shaft because it sounds better is common, and costs distance and accuracy. Let the launch monitor decide.

> [!drill] Lie check at home
> Draw a vertical line on the back of a range ball with a marker. Hit it with an iron with the line facing the clubface. A line printed straight up means a good lie; tilted towards the toe means the club is too upright; towards the heel, too flat. Check with a fitter before bending anything.
