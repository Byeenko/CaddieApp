> [!key] Key points
> - Practise **landing spots**, not just "chipping at the hole".
> - Vary **lies, distances and clubs**. Real rounds never give you the same chip twice.
> - Always **putt out** in games. Getting up and down is what counts.
> - Keep score so you can track improvement.

## Technique drills

**Towel landing spot (chipping)**
Lay a towel about a metre onto the green. Chip ten balls with three different clubs, trying to land each on the towel. Watch how far each club rolls out.

**Line in the sand (bunkers)**
Draw a line in the sand at right angles to the target. Make swings that enter the sand on the line. Then put a ball 5 cm in front of the line and do the same (see [[greenside-bunker-shot]]).

**Clock ladder (pitching)**
Hit five balls each at the 7:30, 9:00 and 10:30 backswing lengths (see [[pitching-basics]]). Note the average carry for each. That's your wedge chart.

## Games (with scoring)

**Up-and-down 9**
Drop a ball at nine different spots around the green: fringe, rough, bunker, uphill, downhill. Play each one and putt out. Score 1 point for every up-and-down (in two shots). Track your score; 3+ is solid, 5+ is very good.

**Par 18**
Nine chips from varied spots, each counting as a par 2: chip and putt. Total score over the nine "holes". Try to beat 18.

**Worst ball**
Hit two chips from the same spot, then play the **worse** one and putt it out. It builds consistency and pressure resistance.

> [!mistake] Same chip, same spot, 50 times
> Hitting the same chip over and over feels productive but rarely transfers. Move after every one or two balls.

> [!tip] Short game first
> If you only have 30 minutes, spend it here. It's where most golfers save the most shots for the least effort.
