> [!key] Key points
> - **Mirror work** with slow swings builds positions faster than hitting balls.
> - A **putting mat** is perfect for start line and short putts.
> - **Simulators** are brilliant for distances, data and random practice, but test what you learn on grass.
> - Little and often beats one long session.

## At home (no ball)
- **Mirror swings:** set up side-on and face-on to a mirror. Make slow swings, pausing at the checkpoints: takeaway, top, impact, finish. Ten slow reps a day changes positions quickly.
- **Grip and setup:** practise taking your grip and setup in front of a mirror (see [[the-grip]] and [[setup-and-posture]]).
- **Tempo:** swing an alignment stick or club to a metronome (see [[tempo-and-rhythm]]).
- **Speed training:** short sessions with a light stick, if you're following a programme (see [[strength-and-speed]]).
- **Putting:** a mat or the carpet with a gate and a coin (see [[putting-drills]]).

## On a simulator
A simulator or launch monitor gives you **instant, honest feedback**. Make the most of it:

| Use it for | How |
| --- | --- |
| **Mapping distances** | Five balls per club; record median carry (see [[knowing-your-distances]]) |
| **Face and path** | Watch face angle, club path and face-to-path while working on a fault (see [[ball-flight-laws]]) |
| **Random practice** | Play a full round on a course, changing club and target every shot |
| **Pressure** | Play a closest-to-the-pin or long-drive game against a mate |
| **Wedges** | Dial in partial-swing distances to exact yardages |

## Simulator caveats
- **Mats are forgiving** of fat shots: the club bounces off and the shot can still look good. Watch smash factor and carry consistency, and check your strike with impact tape.
- **Short game and putting** on a simulator are approximations. Real grass, slopes and lies matter.
- **Ball data vs club data:** some launch monitors measure club data directly, while others estimate it. Treat estimated numbers as a guide.

> [!mistake] Endless drives on the sim
> It's tempting to just bomb drivers on a simulator. A focused 30-minute session with a plan beats an hour of long-drive contests.

> [!drill] Simulator round with a twist
> Play nine holes on the sim. For every approach, call your target and club before you hit, and use your full routine. Count how many times you were within 10% of your intended distance. That's a real skill score, not just a sim score.
