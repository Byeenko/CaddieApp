> [!key] Key points
> - Putting has three skills: **start line**, **speed** and **reading**. Drill each one.
> - **Short putts:** gate drills for start line.
> - **Long putts:** ladder and fringe drills for speed.
> - Finish every session with a **pressure drill**.

## Start line
**Gate drill.** Two tees just wider than the ball, 15–20 cm in front of it on a 1.5-metre straight putt. Roll ten putts through without touching either tee. Narrow the gate as you improve.

**Coin strike.** "Putt" a small coin so it slides cleanly without tumbling, which needs a square face and a centred strike.

**Chalk line or string.** Putt along a straight line. You'll see instantly if the ball starts off line.

## Speed
**Ladder.** Tees at 6, 9, 12 and 15 metres. Putt one ball to each, then back down. Each putt must finish past the last but short of the next (see [[lag-putting]]).

**Fringe drill.** Putt from 10–15 metres and try to stop each ball on the far fringe without running off the green.

**One-metre circle.** Putt ten balls from 12 metres. Score one point each time the ball finishes inside a 1-metre circle around the hole.

## Pressure
**Circle drill.** Six balls in a circle 1 metre from the hole. Hole them all in a row; miss one and start again. Move out to 1.5 metres when it's easy (see [[holing-short-putts]]).

**Around the world.** Four balls at 1, 1.5, 2 and 2.5 metres on four sides of the hole. Make all of them to "complete the world"; miss and restart that side.

**Two-putt challenge.** Nine long putts from different spots. You must two-putt (or better) every one, or start again.

## At home
A carpet or a putting mat is enough for start line and short-putt practice. Speed is hard to practise indoors, but stroke length and tempo work well with a metronome (see [[practising-at-home-and-on-a-simulator]]).

> [!tip] Ten minutes before the round
> Five long putts for speed, five short ones to see the ball go in, then stop. Don't finish your warm-up on a missed putt.
