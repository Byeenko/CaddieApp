> [!key] Key points
> - Most amateurs come up **short**, so when in doubt **take the longer club**.
> - Let **trouble decide**: if danger is long, take the shorter club; if danger is short, take the longer one.
> - Take the longer club and **swing smooth**, or **grip down**.
> - **Commit.** A half-hearted swing with either club is the worst choice.

## The default: longer club, smooth swing
A smooth swing with more club gives a better strike, less spin and a more predictable distance than forcing a short club. That's why tour caddies so often say "take one more".

## Let the trouble decide

| Trouble is… | Choose… |
| --- | --- |
| In front of the green (water, bunker) | The **longer** club. Long is safe |
| Behind the green (out of bounds, steep run-off) | The **shorter** club. Short is safe |
| On both sides, or none | The longer club with a smooth swing |
| The pin is at the back | The longer club |
| The pin is at the front, open entrance | The shorter club, landing short and running on |

## Three ways to play the longer club
1. **Grip down** about 2–3 cm: takes off a few yards and adds control.
2. **Three-quarter swing**, with a matching finish.
3. **Narrow your stance:** shortens the swing naturally.

Pick one method and practise it so you know exactly how many yards it takes off.

## Other factors
- **Adrenaline:** later in a round, or under pressure, many golfers hit it further. Factor it in.
- **The lie:** from a flyer lie in the rough, the shorter club may be right (see [[playing-from-the-rough]]).
- **Your shape:** a draw tends to run more, a fade lands softer.

> [!mistake] Changing your mind over the ball
> Standing over the 7-iron thinking "maybe the 6 was right" leads to a tentative swing. Decide, then commit fully. A committed swing with the "wrong" club beats a doubtful swing with the right one.

> [!drill] Know your knock-down
> On the range or simulator, hit five normal 8-irons, then five 7-irons gripped down with a three-quarter swing. Compare the carry numbers. Now you know exactly what the knock-down 7 does, and you'll trust it on the course.
