> [!key] Key points
> - At address: roughly **50/50**.
> - In the backswing, pressure moves **into the trail foot**, staying on its inside edge.
> - Pressure starts moving **to the lead foot before the club reaches the top**.
> - At impact around **80%** is on the lead side; at the finish, almost all of it.

## Weight vs pressure
Coaches often say "pressure" rather than "weight". Your body doesn't slide from foot to foot like a sack of potatoes. You **push into the ground** harder with one foot than the other. Pressure plates used in coaching show a very consistent pattern in good players: the animation above follows it.

## The pattern, step by step

| Position | Pressure (trail : lead) | What's happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address | 50 : 50 | Balanced, over the middle of both feet |
| Takeaway | 60 : 40 | Turning loads the trail side |
| Top | 70 : 30 or so | Loaded into the inside of the trail foot, not the outside |
| Early downswing | 35 : 65 | **Pressure shifts to the lead side first.** This starts the downswing |
| Impact | 20 : 80 | Firmly on the lead side; hips open |
| Finish | 10 : 90 | Balanced on the lead foot, trail foot up on its toe |

The biggest move is the **shift to the lead side at the start of the downswing**. Good players begin it while the arms are still finishing the backswing. That's what moves the low point in front of the ball (see [[ball-first-contact]]).

> [!mistake] The reverse pivot
> Leaning towards the target in the backswing, then falling back in the downswing, is the reverse of the correct pattern. It produces weak slices, fat shots and a finish where you fall backwards. Feel your trail hip move *back* (not sideways) as you turn.

> [!mistake] Swaying
> Sliding your hips away from the target so your weight goes to the outside of the trail foot. You then have to slide back, and timing becomes very hard. Keep pressure on the inside of the trail foot.

> [!drill] Step drill
> Start with your feet together. As you swing back, step your lead foot towards the target, and swing down as it lands. It forces the lead-side shift at exactly the right time. Start with half swings.

> [!drill] Hold the finish
> If your weight has transferred properly, you can hold your finish balanced on your lead foot for three seconds, with your trail toe barely touching the ground. See [[the-finish]].
