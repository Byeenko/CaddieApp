> [!key] Key points
> - A slice happens when the **clubface is open to the swing path** at impact.
> - It's usually a **combination**: an open face plus an out-to-in ("over the top") path.
> - Fix it in order: **grip → alignment → ball position → path → release**.
> - Fix the **face first**. Fixing the path with the face still open turns a slice into a push.

## What causes a slice

For a right-hander, a slice starts left or straight and curves hard to the right. Two things combine to produce it (see [[ball-flight-laws]]):

- **Clubface:** open (pointing right) relative to where the club is travelling.
- **Swing path:** cutting across the ball from out to in, travelling left.

The bigger the gap between face and path, the bigger the curve. That's why the slice is usually worst with the driver: it has the least loft, so sidespin has the biggest effect.

## Step 1: Check your grip

A weak grip (hands turned too far left) makes it hard to square the face. **Turn both hands slightly to the right** until you see two to three knuckles on your left hand. This is often the quickest fix of all. See [[the-grip]].

## Step 2: Check your alignment

Slicers usually aim their **shoulders left**, often without realising. That sends the club across the ball. Lay an alignment stick along your toes and check your shoulders are parallel to it. See [[alignment]].

## Step 3: Check ball position

If the ball is **too far forward**, your shoulders open up to reach it. That cuts across it. Move the ball back slightly, especially with irons. See [[ball-position]].

## Step 4: Fix the path

The out-to-in path comes from starting the downswing with the shoulders. Feel the lower body start the downswing and the arms **drop** before they swing out. See [[the-downswing]].

> [!drill] Headcover gate
> Place a headcover (or an empty water bottle) just outside the ball and a few centimetres behind it, on the far side from you. If you swing over the top, you'll hit it. Swing from the inside and miss it. Start with half swings.

## Step 5: Let it release

Many slicers hold the face open through impact by keeping their forearms stiff. Feel the right hand **roll over** the left through the ball, so the toe of the club turns over.

> [!drill] Split-hand drill
> Grip the club with your hands a few centimetres apart. Make half swings and feel the right forearm rotate over the left after impact. Hit some balls like this, then go back to your normal grip and keep the feeling.

> [!mistake] Aiming further left
> It's tempting to aim further left to allow for the slice. That makes the path even more out-to-in, so the slice gets worse. It's a downward spiral. Aim straight and fix the cause.

> [!tip] What progress looks like
> As you fix it, the slice usually becomes a **pull** first: face closer to square, path still out to in. That's good news, because the face is fixed. Now work on the path, and the pull becomes a straight shot or a gentle draw.
