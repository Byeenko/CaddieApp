> [!key] Key points
> - Turn all the way through until your **chest faces the target** or a little left of it.
> - Finish with almost all your **weight on your lead foot**, the trail foot up on its toe.
> - Both arms **extend after impact** before folding.
> - **Hold the finish** for three seconds. If you can't, you swung out of balance.

## Why the finish matters

By the time you reach the finish the ball has gone, so it can't change the shot. It's still worth working on for two reasons:

1. **It reflects what happened before it.** A balanced, rotated finish usually means the body led the swing and the weight moved forward. Falling backwards usually means it didn't.
2. **Aiming for a good finish improves the swing.** If you commit to finishing tall and balanced, your body tends to rotate properly through impact to get there.

## The release

After impact your forearms **rotate naturally**: the right hand rolls over the left as the toe of the club turns over. Both arms extend fully down the target line just after impact, then fold up as the body keeps turning.

The release isn't something to force. It happens when your grip pressure is relaxed and the body keeps rotating.

## The finish position

| Check | What you want |
| --- | --- |
| Chest and belt buckle | Facing the target or slightly left |
| Weight | About 90% on the lead foot |
| Trail foot | Up on its toe, laces facing the target |
| Body | Tall, with the spine fairly upright, not bent backwards |
| Hands | Up around your lead shoulder or above your head |

> [!mistake] The chicken wing
> If your lead elbow bends and points out towards the target just after impact, it's called a "chicken wing". It usually happens when the body stops rotating and the arms have nowhere to go. The face often stays open, so you get weak shots to the right. Keep turning your chest through and the arms will fold properly.

```figure

good-label: Both arms extended

```

> [!mistake] Hanging back
> Finishing with your weight on your trail foot (leaning back as if trying to lift the ball) causes thin and fat shots. Your belt buckle should end up ahead of where the ball was.


> [!drill] Hold for three
> Every ball on the range, hold your finish and count to three before moving. Wobbling, stepping or falling means the swing was out of balance, often because you swung too hard. It's simple, and it improves your [[tempo-and-rhythm|tempo]] fast.
