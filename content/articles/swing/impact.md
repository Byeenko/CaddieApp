> [!key] Key points
> - With irons, the **hands are ahead of the ball** at impact and the shaft leans towards the target.
> - Most of your **weight is on your lead foot**.
> - **Hips are open** to the target; shoulders are nearly square.
> - Hit the **ball first, then the turf**. The divot starts on the target side of the ball.

## Why impact is different from address

Many golfers try to return to their address position at impact. Good players don't. At impact the body has rotated and shifted forward, so the position looks quite different from setup.

| | At address | At impact (irons) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weight | Balanced, about 50/50 | Most of it (roughly 70–80%) on the lead foot |
| Hips | Square | Open to the target |
| Shoulders | Square | Nearly square, slightly open |
| Hands | About level with the ball | Ahead of the ball, over the lead thigh |
| Shaft | About vertical | Leaning towards the target |
| Trail heel | Down | Starting to lift |

## Ball first, then turf

With an iron, the lowest point of your swing should be **in front of the ball**. That means the club is still travelling down when it meets the ball. It squeezes the ball against the face ("compression") and then takes a shallow divot after it.

This is why you **hit down to make the ball go up**. The loft on the club does the lifting. Trying to help the ball into the air by scooping it is the cause of most thin and fat shots.

> [!note] The driver is different
> The driver is hit off a tee with the ball forward, so you want to strike it slightly **on the way up**, with little or no forward shaft lean. See [[ball-position]].

> [!mistake] Flipping
> If the trail wrist unhinges before impact, the clubhead overtakes the hands. That adds loft and moves the low point behind the ball. The usual results are weak, high shots, fat shots, or thin ones off the bottom of the club. Leading with the body (see [[the-downswing]]) keeps the hands ahead.

> [!drill] The line drill
> On a practice area (or in a bunker), draw a straight line in the sand or turf, or lay a line of tee pegs. Put balls right on the line. Make swings where your divot or sand mark **starts on the target side of the line**. When it starts behind the line, your low point is too far back. This is the fastest way to see where you're bottoming out.

> [!drill] Towel drill
> Lay a folded towel about 10 cm behind the ball. Hit the ball without touching the towel. If you hit the towel, your low point is behind the ball.

> [!tip] On a simulator mat
> Mats are forgiving of slightly fat shots because the club bounces off them, so some strikes feel better than they would on grass. Watch your launch and spin numbers. A fat shot usually shows lower ball speed for the club and inconsistent carry distance.
