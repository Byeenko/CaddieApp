> [!key] Key points
> - The grip mostly controls the **clubface**, and the clubface is the biggest factor in where the ball starts and how it curves.
> - Hold the club in the **fingers** of the lead hand, not the palm.
> - Start with a **neutral-to-slightly-strong** grip: two to three knuckles visible on your lead hand.
> - Grip pressure should be **firm but not tight**, around 4 out of 10.

## Why the grip matters

Your hands are the only part of you that touches the club. Whatever the rest of the swing does, the grip decides how the clubface sits when it gets back to the ball.

At impact, the clubface angle is the main thing that decides where the ball **starts**. The difference between the face and the swing path decides how it **curves** (see [[ball-flight-laws|ball-flight laws]]). A grip that leaves the face open makes it very hard to stop a slice, no matter how good the rest of your swing is.

## The lead hand (left hand)

1. Let the club rest **diagonally across your fingers**, from the base of your index finger to just under the pad at the heel of your hand.
2. Close the hand so the **heel pad sits on top of the grip**. This gives you leverage and lets your wrist hinge properly.
3. Rest your thumb just to the right of centre on the grip.
4. Look down: you should see **two to three knuckles**. The "V" between your thumb and index finger should point roughly between your chin and right shoulder.

> [!mistake] Holding it in the palm
> Running the grip up the middle of your palm feels secure but stops the wrist from hinging. That usually leaves the face open at impact, which means weak shots that slice. Keep it in the fingers.

## The trail hand (right hand)

1. Hold the club in the **fingers** of the right hand, just below the left.
2. Fold the hand over so the **lifeline of your right palm covers your left thumb**.
3. The "V" of the right hand should point to about the same place as the left: between your chin and right shoulder.
4. Squeeze the trigger finger lightly around the grip. Don't let it point down the shaft.

## Three ways to connect your hands

| Grip | How it works | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Overlapping | Right little finger rests in the gap between left index and middle fingers | Most golfers; the most common grip on tour |
| Interlocking | Right little finger hooks between left index and middle fingers | Smaller hands, or anyone who likes the hands feeling "locked" together |
| Ten-finger | All ten fingers on the grip, like a baseball bat | Beginners, juniors, or players who lack hand strength |

None of these is "correct". Pick the one that feels most secure and lets your hands work as one unit.

> [!tour] On tour
> Most tour players use the overlapping grip, but two of the greatest ever, Jack Nicklaus and Tiger Woods, both interlock. Dustin Johnson plays with a noticeably strong lead hand, matched with a bowed left wrist at the top. Different grips work as long as the rest of the swing suits them.

## Strong, neutral and weak

"Strong" and "weak" describe how far the hands are rotated on the grip. They have nothing to do with how hard you squeeze.

| Grip | What you see | Tends to produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strong | Hands turned to the right; three or more knuckles visible | Face closes more easily: draws, or hooks if overdone |
| Neutral | Two to three knuckles; Vs point at the right shoulder or chin | Square face with a normal release |
| Weak | Hands turned to the left; one knuckle or fewer visible | Face stays open: fades, or slices if overdone |

If you fight a slice, strengthening your grip slightly is often the quickest improvement you can make. Turn both hands a little to the right until you see a third knuckle.

## Grip pressure

On a scale of 1 to 10, aim for about **4**: firm enough to control the club, loose enough that your forearms stay relaxed. Tension in the hands spreads into the arms and shoulders, slows the club down, and stops the face from releasing.

Keep the pressure **the same throughout the swing**. Most golfers grip tighter at the top or in the downswing without noticing.

> [!drill] Grip check routine
> 1. Hold the club out in front of you at waist height, shaft parallel to the ground, with the leading edge pointing straight up.
> 2. Take your left-hand grip. Check the knuckles and the V.
> 3. Add your right hand. Check its V points the same way.
> 4. Lower the club to the ground without letting the hands twist.
>
> Do this ten times before every practice session until it feels automatic.
