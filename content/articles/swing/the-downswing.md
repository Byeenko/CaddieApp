> [!key] Key points
> - Start down with the **lower body**: shift pressure to your lead side and begin to unwind your hips.
> - The order is **hips → chest → arms → club**.
> - Starting with the shoulders throws the club **over the top**, the root cause of most slices.
> - Hold some **wrist hinge** into the downswing. Don't throw it from the top.

## The sequence

Good players move in a consistent order, sometimes called the **kinematic sequence**:

1. **Lower body**: pressure moves into the lead foot as the hips start to unwind. This often begins just before the club has finished going back.
2. **Torso**: the chest follows the hips round.
3. **Arms**: the arms drop and follow the body.
4. **Club**: last of all, the club releases through the ball.

Each part speeds up and then slows down, passing its speed on to the next, like cracking a whip. That's where effortless distance comes from.

## Over the top

If the **shoulders start the downswing**, they swing the arms and club outwards, away from your body. The club then cuts across the ball from out to in.

- With an open face, you get a **slice**.
- With a square or closed face, you get a **pull** left.

This is the most common swing fault in amateur golf. Almost every fix for it starts with getting the lower body to lead.

> [!tip] The feeling
> Many golfers find it helps to feel that the arms simply **drop** at the start of the downswing while the hips turn. Imagine your hands falling down towards your right pocket. The club then approaches the ball from inside the target line.

## Keeping the angle

As you start down, keep some of the wrist hinge you created in the backswing. Releasing it early ("casting") wastes the stored speed before impact, adds loft, and produces weak, high shots or fat ones. You don't need to hold the hinge on purpose. Leading with the body tends to keep it naturally.

> [!drill] Step-through drill
> Set up with your feet together. Swing to the top, then **step your lead foot towards the target** to start the downswing and swing through. It forces the lower body to lead. Start with half swings and short irons.

> [!drill] Pump drill
> Swing to the top. Start down by shifting into your lead side and let your hands drop to about hip height, then go back up to the top. Repeat that "pump" twice, then swing through on the third. It trains the arms to drop rather than throw out.

> [!mistake] Hitting from the top
> Trying to hit hard at the start of the downswing makes the hands and shoulders take over. Save your speed for the bottom. Feel smooth at the start of the downswing and fast through impact.
