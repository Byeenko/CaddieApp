> [!key] Key points
> - **Bend from the hips**, not the waist, with a flat back and a little knee flex.
> - Let your **arms hang** straight down from your shoulders.
> - Stand **shoulder-width** for a mid-iron: narrower for wedges, wider for the driver.
> - Keep your weight **balanced over the middle of your feet**.

## Why setup matters

Your setup decides what your swing can do. Slumped posture stops your body from turning. Standing too close cramps your arms. Weight on your toes pulls you off balance. Most golfers try to fix these problems mid-swing, and you can't. Get the setup right and the swing has room to work.

## Building your posture

1. **Stand tall** with the club held out in front of you at chest height.
2. **Hinge forward from the hips.** Push your backside back as if you were closing a car door with it, keeping your back fairly straight. Lower the club until it touches the ground.
3. **Flex your knees** slightly, just enough to feel athletic. You're not sitting in a chair.
4. **Let your arms hang.** Your hands should sit roughly under your shoulders, about a hand-width away from your thighs.
5. **Tilt slightly away from the target.** Because your right hand is lower on the grip, your right shoulder sits a little lower than your left. It's a small tilt with irons and more with the driver.

> [!tip] The quick check
> From side-on (use your phone camera), draw an imaginary line down from the front of your shoulders. It should run past your knees and land around the balls of your feet. If it lands in front of your toes you're reaching; if it lands on your laces you're too upright or too close.

## Stance width

| Club | Stance width |
| --- | --- |
| Wedges | About hip-width |
| Mid irons | About shoulder-width (insides of the heels in line with the outsides of the shoulders) |
| Long irons and woods | A touch wider than shoulders |
| Driver | Wider again, for a stable base and an upward strike |

Turn your lead foot out a little, about a quarter turn towards the target. That makes it easier to rotate through to a full finish.

## Weight distribution

- **Irons:** roughly 50/50 between your feet, balanced over the middle of each foot.
- **Driver:** a little more on your trail side (about 60/40), which goes with the spine tilt and helps you hit up on the ball.
- **Heels to toes:** you should feel you could bounce lightly on the spot. Not on your heels, not tipping onto your toes.

> [!mistake] Rounded back and locked knees
> Hunching over the ball with straight legs feels comfortable but blocks your turn. Your arms end up doing all the work. A flatter back and soft knees let the big muscles power the swing.

> [!drill] Mirror setup
> Set up side-on to a mirror or your phone camera. Check four things: flat back, slight knee flex, arms hanging under the shoulders, and the shoulder line landing on the balls of your feet. Hold it for five seconds, step away, and do it again. Ten reps a day builds the feel quickly.
