> [!key] Key points
> - Tempo is the **ratio** between backswing time and downswing time, not how fast you swing.
> - Many tour players swing at about **3 to 1**: the backswing takes three times as long as the downswing.
> - **Smooth from the top**, then speed up into the ball.
> - Your tempo should be the **same for every club**, including under pressure.

## Tempo vs speed

Fast swingers and slow swingers can both have great tempo. Tempo is about **proportions**: how long the backswing takes compared with the downswing.

In the book *Tour Tempo*, John Novosel measured tour players' swings frame by frame. He found that most of them took about **three times as long going back as coming down**, whether their overall swing was quick or slow. That 3:1 ratio has become a widely used guide.

> [!tour] On tour
> Ernie Els, nicknamed "the Big Easy", is the classic example of a swing that looks slow and effortless but hits it a long way. Nick Price swung much faster overall. Both had excellent tempo, just at different speeds.

## Rhythm in the transition

The most common tempo fault is **rushing the start of the downswing**. Golfers swing back smoothly and then lunge at the ball from the top. That throws the sequence out of order (see [[the-downswing]]) and costs both distance and accuracy.

Think **smooth at the top, fast at the bottom**. The clubhead should be travelling fastest just after the ball, not at the start of the downswing.

## Finding your tempo

- **Count it.** Say "one-two-three" quietly as you swing back and "four" as you swing through to impact.
- **Swing at 80%.** Most golfers hit it more solidly, and often further, at about 80% effort. Centred strikes beat raw speed.
- **Match your natural pace.** If you walk and talk quickly, your swing will probably suit a quicker tempo. Don't force a slow swing if it isn't you; just keep the ratio.

> [!drill] Feet together
> Hit half to three-quarter shots with your feet touching. Swing too hard and you'll lose your balance straight away. It quickly teaches a smooth, connected rhythm.

> [!drill] Metronome
> Use a free metronome app. Find a beat where your takeaway starts on one click, you reach the top on the next, and you hit through on the third. Practise swings to that rhythm, then hit balls. It's also great for putting.

> [!mistake] Speeding up under pressure
> Nerves make most golfers swing quicker, especially from the top. Make tempo part of your [[pre-shot-routine]]: one smooth rehearsal swing with the same count you use on the range.
