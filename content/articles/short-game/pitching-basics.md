> [!key] Key points
> - A pitch spends **more time in the air than on the ground**.
> - The setup is **between a chip and a full swing**: ball centre, a little weight forward, more wrist hinge.
> - **Turn your body** through the shot. Don't just swing the arms.
> - Control distance with **backswing length**, not by changing speed.

## When to pitch

Pitch when you need to carry something first (rough, a bunker, a slope) or when there isn't enough green to let a chip run out. It's also the shot for those awkward 30–70 yard distances that are too short for a full wedge.

## The setup

1. **Stance**: a bit narrower than a full swing, slightly open.
2. **Ball position**: centre of your stance.
3. **Weight**: slightly favouring the lead side, about 55–60%.
4. **Hands**: level with the ball or a fraction ahead. Less shaft lean than a chip, so you keep the club's loft and bounce working.

## The swing

- **Hinge the wrists** on the way back. More than a chip, less than a full swing.
- **Rotate your chest** back and through. The body controls the club, so a pitch is a mini golf swing, not an arm swing.
- **Accelerate** through the ball to a finish that matches your backswing.
- Let the **bounce** (the rounded sole of the wedge) brush the turf. You don't need to dig.

## The clock system

Picture yourself standing in the middle of a clock face, with your lead arm as the hand of the clock. Make backswings that stop at set points:

```figure
type: clock
```

| Backswing | Lead arm points to |
| --- | --- |
| Short | About 7:30 |
| Medium | 9:00 (lead arm parallel to the ground) |
| Long | About 10:30 |

Keep the **same tempo** every time and just change the length. This approach was made popular by short-game coach Dave Pelz.

> [!drill] Build your wedge chart
> On the range or simulator, hit 10 balls with each wedge at each clock position (7:30, 9:00, 10:30). Write down the **average carry** in yards. Three wedges times three swings gives you nine reliable distances. Add them to your phone notes or your yardage book. The Caddie app will hold your chart in a later phase.

> [!mistake] Decelerating
> A big backswing followed by slowing down into the ball causes chunks and skulls. If you have to slow down, the backswing was too long. Shorten it and accelerate through.

> [!tip] Landing and stopping
> A pitch lands and checks up more than a chip, but on firm greens it can still roll a long way. Look at how the greens are playing before your round. Firm greens mean aiming to land shorter.

From sand, the technique changes completely. See [[greenside-bunker-shot]].
