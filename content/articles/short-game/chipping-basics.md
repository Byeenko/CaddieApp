> [!key] Key points
> - A chip spends **a little time in the air and a lot on the ground**, like a long putt.
> - **Narrow stance, weight forward, ball back of centre**, hands slightly ahead.
> - Use a **putting-style stroke** driven by the shoulders, with very little wrist.
> - **Pick a landing spot** and change clubs to control how much it rolls.

## When to chip

Chip when you're just off the green, the lie is decent, and there's nothing to carry except a little fringe or rough. Rolling the ball is far more predictable than flying it, so the general rule is: **get it on the ground as soon as you can**.

## The setup

1. **Narrow stance**, feet a few inches apart, slightly open (lead foot pulled back a touch).
2. **Ball back of centre**, around the inside of your trail foot.
3. **Weight on your lead side**, about 60–70%, and it stays there throughout the shot.
4. **Hands slightly ahead** of the ball, so the shaft leans a little towards the target.
5. **Grip down** the handle for control, and stand closer to the ball.

## The stroke

Rock your shoulders back and through, as you would on a long putt. The wrists stay quiet and the body turns gently through. The club strikes **down slightly** on the ball, and the loft pops it up.

Keep the **backswing and follow-through about the same length**, and keep the speed even. Never decelerate into the ball.

> [!mistake] Trying to lift it
> The most common chipping error is trying to help the ball into the air by flicking the wrists or leaning back. The low point moves behind the ball and you hit it fat or thin. Trust the loft: hands ahead, weight forward, hit down.

## Choosing the club

Pick a **landing spot** about a metre onto the green, where the surface is consistent. Then choose the club that will land there and roll out to the hole.

As a rough guide on a flat green at normal speed, the ball's air time to roll time looks like this:

| Club | Carry : roll |
| --- | --- |
| Sand wedge | About 1 : 1 |
| Pitching wedge | About 1 : 2 |
| 9-iron | About 1 : 3 |
| 8-iron | About 1 : 4 |
| 7-iron | About 1 : 5 |

Uphill, fast or downhill greens change this, so test it on your own course's practice green. Many good chippers use **one stroke and change the club** rather than changing the stroke.

> [!drill] Landing-spot towel
> Lay a small towel about a metre onto the practice green. Chip to a hole using three different clubs, trying to land every ball on the towel. Notice how far each one rolls. Ten minutes of this teaches you more about distance control than an hour of random chipping.

> [!drill] Ladder
> Put tees at 5, 10, 15 and 20 paces from the edge of the green. Chip one ball to each, then work back down. Play it as a game: every ball has to finish past the previous one but short of the next tee.

When a chip won't do it, it's time for a [[pitching-basics|pitch]].
