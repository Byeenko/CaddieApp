> [!key] Key points
> - The **bump-and-run** is low-risk. Use it whenever there's green to work with.
> - The **flop shot** is high-risk. Use it only when you must go high and stop the ball quickly, and the lie is good.
> - Both rely on **setup**, not a different swing speed.
> - Practise them before trying them on the course.

## The bump-and-run
A low shot with a 7-, 8- or 9-iron (or even a hybrid) that lands just on the green and rolls most of the way, like a putt.

**When:** lots of green between you and the hole, a firm surface, or into the wind. Very common on links-style and firm summer courses.

**How:**
1. Set up as for a [[chipping-basics|chip]]: narrow stance, ball back of centre, weight forward, hands ahead.
2. Stand closer and grip down. The club sits more upright, like a putter.
3. Make a **putting-style stroke** with your shoulders and quiet wrists.
4. Land it just onto the green and let it run.

## The flop shot
A very high, soft shot that lands and stops almost straight away.

**When:** short-sided over a bunker to a tight pin, with a **good lie** (the ball sitting up on grass). Never from a bare or tight lie.

**How:**
1. **Open the face** of your lob or sand wedge so it points almost to the sky, *then* take your grip.
2. **Open your stance**: feet and body aimed well left.
3. **Ball forward**, opposite your lead heel. Weight about 50/50.
4. Make a **long, full-speed swing** along your body line. Let the club slide *under* the ball using the bounce.
5. **Don't stop.** Keep the face open and rotate through to a full finish.

> [!mistake] Decelerating on a flop
> The big swing for a short shot feels wrong, so golfers slow down. The leading edge then catches the ball and it shoots across the green. If you're not willing to swing through, don't play the flop. Chip to the fat side instead.

> [!warning] Know when not to
> A flop from a tight lie risks a shank, a skull or a duff. A chip or putt to 6 metres is a better percentage play than a flop that might finish anywhere.

## Other options worth knowing
- **Putter from off the green** (the "Texas wedge"): smooth fringe, no obstacles. Usually the safest option.
- **Hybrid chip:** from the collar of rough against the fringe. Putting stroke; the ball pops up and rolls.
- **Belly the wedge:** ball against the edge of rough? Use a sand wedge's leading edge to "putt" the ball at its equator.

> [!drill] Same spot, three shots
> Drop five balls in one spot off the green. Play a bump-and-run, a standard chip and a flop to the same hole, and note where each finishes. You'll quickly learn which shot is reliable for you, and when.
