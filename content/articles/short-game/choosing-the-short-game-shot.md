> [!key] Key points
> - The **lower the shot, the more reliable** it is.
> - Run through the order: **putt → chip → pitch → lob**. Choose the first one that works.
> - Decide based on the **lie**, what you have to **carry**, and how much **green** you have to work with.
> - Your *worst* putt is usually closer than your average chip.

## The ladder

There's an old saying in golf: *a bad putt is usually better than a bad chip, and a bad chip is better than a bad pitch.* Each step up the ladder adds air time, and air time adds risk: more chance of a poor strike, more spin to judge, and more bounce to guess.

1. **Putt** if the grass between you and the green is short and smooth.
2. **Chip** if you need to carry a little fringe or rough, but have room to let the ball roll.
3. **Pitch** if you need to carry something, or the pin is close to your edge of the green.
4. **Lob or flop** only when you have no other choice: over a bunker to a tight pin, for example.

## Four questions to ask

**1. What's the lie?**
- Sitting up nicely: anything is possible.
- Tight or bare: putt or chip. A high, open-faced shot is risky.
- In thick rough: you'll need loft and a steeper swing to get the club through the grass.

**2. What do I have to carry?**
- Only fringe: putt or chip.
- Rough, a slope or a bunker: pitch.

**3. How much green is there between the edge and the hole?**
- Lots of green: chip with a less-lofted club and let it run.
- Very little: you need more loft so the ball stops quickly.

**4. What's the slope?**
- Uphill to the hole: the ball slows quickly, so a running shot is easy.
- Downhill: the ball runs out. Land it as softly as you can, as early as you can.

## Quick reference

| Situation | Shot |
| --- | --- |
| Fairway lie, short fringe, lots of green | Putt (the "Texas wedge") |
| Off the green, 5–15 m of green to the hole | Chip with a 9-iron or PW |
| Off the green, pin close to your edge | Chip with a sand wedge, or a short pitch |
| Rough or slope to carry | Pitch |
| Bunker between you and the pin | Pitch over it to the middle; get greedy only if the lie is good |
| Ball in a greenside bunker | Splash shot (see [[greenside-bunker-shot]]) |

> [!tip] Aim at the safe side
> From a tough spot, don't aim at the pin by default. Getting the ball onto the green and leaving yourself a putt is a win. Short-siding yourself again, or leaving it in the rough, is where the big numbers come from.

> [!drill] Three-ball game
> Drop three balls around the practice green in different spots. For each one, decide your shot using the four questions, then play it. Putt out every ball. Keep score. Over time, aim to get up and down at least one in three.
