> [!key] Key points
> - **Open the clubface** first, then take your grip.
> - **Dig your feet in**, ball forward, weight slightly forward.
> - **Hit the sand** about 2–5 cm behind the ball. The club never touches the ball.
> - **Swing through** to a full finish. Never stop at the sand.

## How it works

Your sand wedge has **bounce**: the sole sits lower than the leading edge. When you open the face and swing into the sand, the bounce **skids through** the sand instead of digging in. A cushion of sand throws the ball out softly. The club doesn't need to touch the ball at all.

That's why you need to commit to **hitting the sand**. It feels wrong, but it's what makes the shot easy.

## The setup

1. **Open the face** so it points up and slightly right, *then* take your grip. Don't grip first and then twist the face open, or it'll close again during the swing.
2. **Open your stance** slightly, aiming your feet and body a little left of the target to allow for the open face.
3. **Wiggle your feet** into the sand for stability. This also lowers the bottom of your swing slightly below the ball.
4. **Ball forward** of centre, around your lead heel.
5. **Weight slightly on the lead side.**
6. **Hover the club** above the sand. Don't touch the sand before the stroke (see the rules note below).

## The swing

- Pick a spot in the sand **about 2–5 cm (an inch or two) behind the ball** and focus on that spot, not the ball.
- Make a **fairly full swing** with plenty of wrist hinge. A bunker shot needs more effort than a pitch of the same distance, because the sand slows the club.
- **Splash** the sand out, swinging along your body line.
- **Finish** with the club up and your chest facing the target.

> [!mistake] Decelerating into the sand
> Fear of thinning it over the green leads to slowing down. The club sticks in the sand and the ball stays in the bunker. Commit to a full follow-through every time.

> [!note] Rules: don't touch the sand
> Under the Rules of Golf (Rule 12.2b) you can't touch the sand with your club **right in front of or behind the ball**, during your backswing, or in a practice swing. In stroke play it's a two-stroke penalty. You can rest the club in the bunker elsewhere, and it's fine to dig your feet in to take your stance.

> [!drill] The line drill
> In a practice bunker, draw a line in the sand at right angles to the target. Without a ball, make swings where the club enters the sand **on the line** and throws sand forward. Once you can do it ten times in a row, put a ball a few centimetres in front of the line and do the same thing.

## Distance control

- **Longer shot:** less open face, and take a little less sand.
- **Shorter shot:** more open face, keep the same swing, and take slightly more sand.
- **Wet or firm sand:** the club bounces more, so square the face slightly. Some players even use a pitching wedge.
- **Soft, fluffy sand:** more open face and a more aggressive swing.

Plugged lies ("fried egg") need a different technique: square or slightly closed face, steeper swing and less follow-through. That one is coming in a future lesson.
