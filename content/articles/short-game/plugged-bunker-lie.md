> [!key] Key points
> - **Square or slightly close** the clubface. No open face this time.
> - Ball **centre to slightly back**, weight on the lead side.
> - A **steep** swing that drives the club **down** into the sand just behind the ball.
> - The ball comes out **low, with little spin, and runs**. Allow for it.

## Why it's different
With a normal bunker shot you open the face so the **bounce** skids through the sand. When the ball is plugged, it sits *below* the surface. An open face just bounces off the top and never gets under it. You need the **leading edge** to dig down and lift the ball out with the sand.

## How to play it
1. **Clubface:** square, or slightly closed (toe turned in). Some players use a pitching wedge for its sharper leading edge.
2. **Stance:** fairly square, feet dug in.
3. **Ball position:** centre or slightly back.
4. **Weight:** 60–70% on the lead side, and it stays there.
5. **Swing:** pick the club up steeply with an early wrist hinge, then hit **down hard**, about 3–5 cm behind the ball.
6. **Finish:** there won't be much follow-through, because the club buries in the sand. That's fine.

## What to expect
- The ball comes out **lower** and with almost **no spin**.
- It **runs** a long way after landing, so land it well short of the hole, or just get it on the green.
- If the pin is close and the lip is high, accept that getting out anywhere on the green is a good result.

> [!mistake] Using the splash technique
> Opening the face and swinging normally from a plugged lie usually leaves the ball in the bunker. Close the face and hit down.

> [!note] Rules reminder
> A ball embedded in a **bunker** gets no free relief: you play it as it lies, or take an unplayable (see [[penalties-and-relief]]). Free relief for an embedded ball applies only in the general area (fairway and rough).

> [!drill] Make your own fried eggs
> In a practice bunker, drop balls from shoulder height so they plug. Play five with the normal splash technique, then five with this one. The difference will convince you.
