> [!key] Key points
> - A round has about **2 minutes of actual hitting** in 4 hours. Concentrate **in short bursts**.
> - **Switch on** at the start of your routine; **switch off** once the shot is over.
> - Focus on **process** (your target, your routine), not outcome (your score).
> - Use a simple **trigger** to switch on, like closing the Velcro on your glove.

## The on/off approach
Trying to concentrate for the whole round wears you out, and by the back nine your focus has gone. Good players treat focus like a light switch:

| Phase | What you're doing |
| --- | --- |
| **Switched off** (most of the round) | Walking, talking, enjoying the course, staying relaxed |
| **Switching on** (about 30 metres from your ball) | Start gathering information: lie, wind, distance |
| **Fully on** (your routine) | Decide, commit, hit, with the [[pre-shot-routine]] |
| **Switching off** (after the shot) | React, accept it, let it go, then back to relaxed |

## What to focus on
- **A small target.** "That tree on the left edge", not "the fairway". A clear target occupies the mind.
- **One feel**, not a checklist. "Smooth tempo" or "turn through", never five swing thoughts.
- **The present shot.** Not the double bogey two holes ago, not the score you'll post.

## Process vs outcome
**Outcome** thoughts ("I need a par here", "I'm two over") create tension. **Process** thoughts ("pick the target, full routine, commit") are the things you can actually control. Set process goals for the round, such as "full routine on every shot" or "commit to every club choice", and judge yourself on those.

> [!mistake] Doing maths on the course
> Adding up your score every hole and working out what you need to finish with is a classic focus killer. Write the scores down, then forget about them until the 18th.

> [!tip] Switch-on trigger
> Choose a small physical action that starts your routine every time: fastening your glove, a deep breath behind the ball, or touching the brim of your cap. Over time, that action switches your focus on automatically.

> [!drill] Process scorecard
> Next round, keep a second score: a tick for every shot where you did your full routine and committed to the shot, whatever the result. Aim for 90%+. Most golfers find their real score improves as the process score goes up.
