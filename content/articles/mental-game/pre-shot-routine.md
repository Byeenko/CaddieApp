> [!key] Key points
> - A routine makes every shot feel familiar, including the ones that matter.
> - Separate **thinking** (choosing the shot) from **doing** (hitting it).
> - Keep it short, about **20–30 seconds** once it's your turn.
> - Do the **same thing every time**, especially under pressure.

## Why a routine works

Every shot you hit is a little different: new lie, new distance, new wind. A routine is the one part that stays the same. It gives your mind and body a familiar path into the swing, so you hit the shot instead of thinking about it.

Golfers with consistent routines tend to cope better with pressure, because the routine gives them something to focus on other than the outcome.

## Think, then play

A popular way to structure a routine comes from the coaches Pia Nilsson and Lynn Marriott (Vision54). They split it into two zones:

- **The think box**, behind the ball: gather information, choose the shot, commit.
- **The play box**, over the ball: no more analysing. Feel the shot and hit it.

Once you step into the play box, you've already decided. If doubt creeps in, step back out and start again.

## Building your routine

1. **Gather information:** distance, lie, wind, hazards, and where the safe miss is.
2. **Choose the shot:** pick the club, the shape and a *specific* target. Choose a tree or a bunker edge, not "the green".
3. **Commit:** say it to yourself: "Smooth 7-iron at the left edge of the green."
4. **Rehearse:** one practice swing that matches the shot you're about to hit.
5. **Align:** from behind the ball, pick an intermediate target a foot or two in front of it. Set the clubface to that spot first, then your feet and body.
6. **Go:** one look at the target, one waggle, and swing. Don't stand over the ball.

> [!tour] On tour
> Jack Nicklaus described "going to the movies" before every shot. He pictured the ball's flight and where it would finish before he set up to it. Visualising the shot is one of the most widely used tools in the game.

## After the shot

What you do *after* a shot matters too. Give yourself a few seconds to react, good or bad, then let it go. Once you start walking, you're thinking about the next shot, not the last one. See [[bouncing-back]] for more.

> [!mistake] Speeding up under pressure
> When nerves kick in, most golfers either rush the routine or add extra checks and practice swings. Both throw off your rhythm. If your routine normally takes 25 seconds, it should take 25 seconds on the 18th with a match on the line too.

> [!drill] One-ball practice
> At the end of every range or simulator session, hit your last 10 balls with your full routine. Change club and target every shot, as if you were playing a real hole. Practising this way is the bridge between range swings and course swings.

## Keeping pace

A good routine is quick. Do your information gathering while your playing partners are hitting, so you're ready when it's your turn. That's "ready golf", and it keeps the round moving.
