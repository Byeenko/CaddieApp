> [!key] Key points
> - Nerves are **normal**. Even tour players feel them. The aim is to play well *with* them.
> - **Slow your breathing** to calm your body.
> - Stick to your **routine** and **tempo**. Pressure makes most people rush.
> - Pick a **conservative target** and a **smooth swing** on big moments.

## What pressure does
Under pressure your body releases adrenaline. Your heart rate goes up, your grip tightens, your muscles tense, and time seems to speed up. The result is usually a **quicker tempo**, a **tighter grip** and **shorter, tense swings**. Knowing this means you can counter each effect directly.

| Pressure effect | Counter |
| --- | --- |
| Faster heart rate, shallow breathing | Slow breathing (below) |
| Tighter grip | Consciously soften the grip to about 4/10 |
| Rushed tempo | Same routine; a slow first move away |
| Racing thoughts | One clear target, one swing feel |
| Adrenaline adds distance | Consider less club on approach shots |

## Breathing
A simple technique is **box breathing**: breathe in for 4 counts, hold for 4, out for 4, hold for 4. Do two or three rounds while you wait on the tee. A long, slow out-breath is the part that settles your body.

## The first tee
- **Warm up properly** so your body is ready (see [[warm-up-and-pre-round]]).
- **Pick a safe club** you trust, not necessarily the driver.
- **Choose a big, safe target** and commit to it.
- **Smooth tempo**: feel like you swing at 80%.
- Remember: nobody is watching as closely as you think.

## Closing out a good round
- **Stop counting.** Play the hole in front of you.
- **Keep the same strategy** that got you there. Don't suddenly protect a lead by steering shots.
- **Expect nerves**, and use your routine to give your mind something to do.

> [!mistake] Changing your routine
> Taking extra practice swings, re-reading putts or hurrying to get it over with. A routine is most valuable exactly when it feels hardest to follow.

> [!tip] Practise under pressure
> Add consequences to practice: hole ten 1-metre putts in a row or start again; play a match against your mate on the short-game green with a small bet. Practising with mild pressure makes real pressure feel more familiar.

> [!note] The yips
> If short putts or chips have become a genuine involuntary twitch, you're not alone; it's a recognised problem. A change of grip (claw, cross-handed) or a golf psychologist can help.
