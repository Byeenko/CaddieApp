> [!key] Key points
> - Golf is a game of **misses**. Expect them, and judge yourself on how you handle them.
> - Base expectations on your **real averages**, not your best day.
> - Set **process goals** for rounds and **skill goals** for practice.
> - **Enjoy it.** You'll play better relaxed than tense.

## Realistic expectations
Golfers often expect to hit most fairways and greens and to hole most short putts. The numbers say otherwise:
- Tour players hit roughly **60%** of fairways and **two-thirds** of greens.
- A typical 15-handicapper hits around **4–5 greens** a round.
- Even tour players make only around **half** of their 2.5-metre putts.

So when you miss a green from 160 yards, that's normal. When you three-putt from 15 metres, that's normal too. Expecting perfection turns normal golf into a string of disappointments.

## Goals that work

| Type | Example | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Process** (on the course) | Full routine on every shot | Fully within your control |
| **Skill** (in practice) | 8 of 10 putts from 1.5 metres | Specific and measurable |
| **Outcome** (over a season) | Get my handicap to 12 | Gives direction; check it monthly, not hourly |

## Mindset habits
- **Trust what you've practised.** The course is for playing, not fixing.
- **Be your own good caddie.** Talk to yourself the way you'd talk to a mate: encouraging, practical, calm.
- **Find the fun.** A great walk, good company, one great shot a round. That's why we play.

> [!tour] Worth reading
> *Golf Is Not a Game of Perfect* by sports psychologist Bob Rotella is one of the best-known books on golf mindset. Its core idea is accepting imperfection and committing to every shot.

> [!mistake] Judging a round by one number
> A round where you followed your plan and hit poor shots is better progress than a lucky good score. Review the **process** as well as the score (see [[reviewing-your-round]]).

> [!drill] Three good things
> After every round, write down three things that went well before you look at what went wrong. It keeps the picture balanced, and tells you what to keep doing.
