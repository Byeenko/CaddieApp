> [!key] Key points
> - Go through the same **five checks** every time: **lie, distance, conditions, trouble, target**.
> - Choose a shot you can hit **seven times out of ten**.
> - **Decide, then commit.** Doubt is more costly than the "wrong" club.
> - Keep it **quick**: gather information while others are playing.

## The five checks

| Check | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Lie** | Clean, in a divot, rough, slope? (see the Lies & Conditions lessons) |
| **2. Distance** | To the front, middle and back; and to any trouble |
| **3. Conditions** | Wind, elevation, temperature, firmness: the "plays like" number (see [[wind-elevation-temperature]]) |
| **4. Trouble** | Where's the big number? Where's the safe miss? (see [[where-to-miss]]) |
| **5. Target** | A specific target, and the shot shape and club that get there most reliably |

Then pick **one club**, **one target**, **one swing feel**, and go.

## The 7-out-of-10 rule
Ask: "If I hit this shot ten times, would at least seven be fine?" If not, choose an easier shot: more club and a smoother swing, a bigger target, or a safer line. Save the hero shots for when the miss is harmless.

## Commit
Once you've decided, the decision is done. Standing over the ball doubting the club leads to a tentative, steering swing. If you genuinely change your mind, step away, re-decide, and start your routine again.

> [!mistake] Deciding on the club before seeing the lie
> Walking to your ball already thinking "7-iron" means you might ignore a flyer lie, a slope or a change in the wind. Look at the lie first.

> [!tip] Talk it through
> Saying your decision out loud ("150, into the breeze, plays 160, 6-iron at the middle, miss left is fine") makes it clear and committed. Playing partners won't mind, and it's how tour players work with their caddies.

> [!drill] Decision journal
> For one round, jot a one-word reason next to each approach shot on your scorecard ("wind", "lie", "safe"). Afterwards, see which decisions worked out. You'll quickly spot patterns, like always under-clubbing into the wind.
