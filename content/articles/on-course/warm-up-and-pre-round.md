> [!key] Key points
> - The warm-up is for **getting ready**, not **fixing your swing**.
> - **Move your body** first, then hit short shots before long ones.
> - Find the **speed of the greens**. It's the most valuable part.
> - Finish by **rehearsing your first tee shot**.

## A 25-minute warm-up
*Adjust the times to what you have.*

| Time | What | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | **Dynamic movement:** arm circles, trunk rotations, hip swings, a few lunges (see [[mobility-for-golf]]) | Loosens the joints the swing needs |
| 8 min | **Range:** wedges (half then full), mid-irons, a few hybrids or woods, 3–4 drivers | Builds from small to big swings; finds your rhythm |
| 2 min | **First tee rehearsal:** 2–3 balls with your opening club and full routine | Ready for the first shot |
| 10 min | **Putting:** long putts for speed, then short ones to finish | Speed of the greens is the biggest scoring factor |

If you only have **10 minutes**, skip the range and do movement plus putting.

## What to look for
- **Your shot shape today.** Fading a bit more than usual? Note it and plan for it. Don't try to fix it.
- **How the greens are rolling:** fast, slow, firm?
- **How the ground is reacting** around the practice green: do chips release or stop?

## On the first tee
- Pick a club you trust and a **big, safe target**.
- Take one **practice swing** at the speed you want.
- **Breathe out slowly**, then go (see [[nerves-and-pressure]]).

> [!mistake] Working on technique before the round
> Changing your swing on the range 20 minutes before teeing off fills your head with thoughts on the course. Play with what you've got today.

> [!mistake] No warm-up at all
> Arriving five minutes before your tee time and hitting driver cold is a common route to a slow start, and to injuries (see [[injury-prevention]]). Even five minutes of movement helps.

> [!tip] Last thing before teeing off
> Finish your putting with three short putts that go in. Walk to the tee having just seen the ball drop.
