> [!key] Key points
> - **Draw** (for a right-hander): starts right of the target and curves back left. **Face closed to the path**, path in-to-out.
> - **Fade:** starts left and curves back right. **Face open to the path**, path out-to-in.
> - Set the **face where you want the ball to start**, and the **path** across it.
> - Have a **stock shape** you rely on, and only shape the other way when you need to.

## How it works
From [[ball-flight-laws]]: the face mostly sets the **start direction**; the face compared with the path sets the **curve**.

## Hitting a draw
1. **Aim your body** (feet, hips, shoulders) along the line you want the ball to **start**: right of the target.
2. **Aim the clubface** between the start line and the target, slightly closed to your body line.
3. **Swing along your body line.** That gives an in-to-out path relative to the target.
4. Let the forearms **release** fully through impact.

## Hitting a fade
1. **Aim your body** left of the target, along the start line.
2. **Aim the clubface** between the start line and the target, slightly open to your body line.
3. **Swing along your body line** (out-to-in relative to the target).
4. Feel the body rotate through while the face stays a touch **open**. Hold off the release slightly.

## Which shape should you play?

| | Draw | Fade |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flight | Lower, more roll | Higher, lands softer |
| Distance | Usually a little longer | Usually a little shorter |
| Control | Harder to stop on firm greens | Easier to stop; often more predictable |
| Classic users | Many long hitters | Many of the best ball-strikers in history |

Neither is better. What matters is having **one reliable shape**. Jack Nicklaus famously played a controlled fade for most of his career.

> [!mistake] Changing the grip to shape it
> Strengthening or weakening your grip for one shot is unreliable. Change your **setup**: aim of body and face. Keep the grip the same.

> [!tip] Use the shape strategically
> With a pin on the right side of the green, a fade lets you aim at the middle and let the ball work towards the flag. If it doesn't curve, you're still on the green.

> [!drill] Nine-shot drill
> On the range, try to hit all nine ball flights: low, medium and high, each as a draw, straight and fade. You won't manage all of them at first, but trying teaches you more about face and path control than any other drill.
