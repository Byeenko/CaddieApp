> [!key] Key points
> - **The clubface mostly decides where the ball starts.**
> - **The face compared with the path decides how it curves.**
> - Face open to the path: the ball curves **right**. Face closed to the path: it curves **left**.
> - Off-centre strikes, especially with woods, also add curve.

## The old laws were wrong

For years golfers were taught that the ball starts in the direction of the **swing path** and curves because of the face. Launch monitors such as Trackman showed it's the other way around. The **face angle is the main influence on start direction**: roughly three-quarters with irons, and even more with the driver.

## The two numbers

**Face angle**: where the clubface points at impact, relative to the target.
- Open = pointing right of target
- Closed = pointing left of target

**Club path**: the direction the club is travelling at impact.
- In to out = travelling right of target
- Out to in = travelling left of target

**Face to path**: the difference between the two. This is what creates curve.

## The nine shots

For a right-handed golfer:

| Start | Curves right | Straight | Curves left |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starts right | Push-slice | Push | Push-draw |
| Starts straight | Fade (slice if big) | Straight | Draw (hook if big) |
| Starts left | Pull-fade (pull-slice if big) | Pull | Pull-hook |

Read it like this:
- **Start direction** comes mostly from the face. If it starts right, the face was open to the target.
- **Curve** comes from the face compared with the path. If it curves right, the face was open to the path.

## Diagnosing your shot

| You see | Face (to target) | Path | Face vs path |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starts left, curves right (classic slice) | Slightly left | Strongly out to in | Open to path |
| Starts right, curves left (draw) | Slightly right | In to out, more right than the face | Closed to path |
| Starts right, no curve (push) | Open | In to out, same as face | Matched |
| Starts left, no curve (pull) | Closed | Out to in, same as face | Matched |

> [!tip] Using this
> When you hit a bad shot, ask two questions. **Where did it start?** That tells you about the face. **Which way did it curve?** That tells you how the path compared with the face. Most golfers try to fix their path when the problem is the face.

## Strike location: gear effect

With drivers and woods, **where on the face** you hit the ball also changes the curve. A strike off the **toe** tends to make the ball curve left (draw). A strike off the **heel** tends to make it curve right (fade). This "gear effect" is why a centred strike matters so much with the driver.

> [!note] Using a simulator or launch monitor
> Most launch monitors show **face angle**, **club path** and **face to path** after every shot. Look for those numbers. A slicer will typically see a negative (out-to-in) path with the face open to it. Fixing a slice starts with getting those two numbers closer together. See [[fixing-a-slice]].
