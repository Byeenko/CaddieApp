> [!key] Key points
> - **Backspin** keeps the ball in the air and stops it on the green.
> - What people call "sidespin" is really a **tilted spin axis**, caused by the face being open or closed to the path.
> - Spin comes mostly from **spin loft** (the gap between where the face points and where the club travels) and **clean contact**.
> - **Grass or moisture** between the face and the ball kills spin.

## Backspin
Every shot spins backwards. The **loft** on the club, combined with a descending strike, makes the ball roll up the face slightly at impact, creating backspin. That backspin creates lift.

| Club | Typical backspin |
| --- | --- |
| Driver | 2,000–3,000 rpm (ideal for most) |
| 7-iron | 6,000–7,000 rpm |
| Wedge | 8,000–10,000+ rpm |

- **Too little spin** with irons: the ball won't hold the green.
- **Too much spin** with the driver: it balloons and loses distance.

## "Sidespin" and spin axis
The ball only spins around one axis. When the face is square to the path, the axis is level and the ball flies straight. When the face is open or closed to the path, the axis **tilts**, and the ball curves the way it's tilted. That's why curve depends on **face-to-path** (see [[ball-flight-laws]]). More backspin means a given tilt curves *less*, which is why drivers curve more than wedges.

## What creates more spin
- **Spin loft:** the difference between dynamic loft (where the face points) and attack angle (the direction the club travels). Hitting down on a wedge with a lofted face creates lots of spin.
- **Clean, dry contact:** clean grooves, a dry ball, a tight lie.
- **Ball:** urethane-covered balls spin more on short shots (see [[choosing-a-golf-ball]]).
- **Club speed:** faster swings create more spin with the same club.

## What kills spin
- **Grass between the ball and face** (flyer lies; see [[playing-from-the-rough]]).
- **Wet conditions** or a dirty clubface.
- **Thin strikes** low on the face.
- **Hitting up** on a wedge or iron (flipping).

> [!mistake] Chasing spin on every chip
> Spinning a chip back is fun on TV, but a predictable release is what gets the ball close. For most chips, plan for some roll and control it.

> [!drill] Spin check on a launch monitor
> Hit ten 7-irons and note the spin numbers. Then hit ten with the ball slightly forward and the hands level, and ten with the ball back and the hands forward. See how setup changes spin, then use that on the course when you need the ball to stop or release.
