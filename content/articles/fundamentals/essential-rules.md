> [!key] Key points
> - **Play the ball as it lies** and the course as you find it.
> - **14 clubs maximum** in your bag.
> - You have **3 minutes** to search for a lost ball. Hit a **provisional** if you think it might be lost or out of bounds.
> - In a bunker, **don't touch the sand** with your club before you play.

> [!note] About these rules
> This is a practical summary of the Rules of Golf (R&A and USGA). For anything unusual, check the official Rules of Golf app. Clubs can also add their own **local rules**, usually printed on the scorecard.

## Before and on the tee
- **14 clubs max.** Carrying more is a penalty for each hole where you had them (capped at 4 strokes in stroke play).
- **Tee the ball** between the tee markers and up to two club-lengths behind them. In stroke play, teeing off in front of the markers costs **2 strokes** and you must replay.
- **Use your own ball.** Put an identifying mark on it with a pen. Playing someone else's ball by mistake is **2 strokes** in stroke play.

## Through the green
- **Play it as it lies.** You can't improve your lie, the area of your stance or swing, or your line of play by pressing down, bending or breaking things.
- **Loose impediments** (leaves, sticks, stones) can be removed anywhere, including bunkers and penalty areas. Be careful: if your ball moves when you do it, that's a 1-stroke penalty (except on the green).
- **Movable obstructions** (rakes, bottles, signs) can be removed anywhere.
- **Advice:** you can only ask your partner or caddie for advice, not other players, about club choice or how to play a shot. Rules and distances aren't advice.

## Lost balls and provisionals
- You have **3 minutes** to search from when you start looking.
- If your ball might be lost outside a penalty area, or out of bounds, hit a **provisional ball** before going forward. Say clearly "I'm playing a provisional". If the first ball turns up in bounds, carry on with it. If not, the provisional is in play with a **1-stroke penalty**. It saves the walk back.
- See [[penalties-and-relief]] for what to do when a ball is lost, out of bounds or unplayable.

## In bunkers
- You **can't touch the sand** with your club right in front of or behind the ball, in a practice swing, or on your backswing. That's 2 strokes in stroke play.
- You **can** dig your feet in, remove loose impediments, and rest a club elsewhere in the bunker.
- Rake the bunker when you leave it (see [[etiquette-and-pace]]).

## On the green
- **Mark, lift and clean** your ball whenever you like. Use a coin or ball marker behind the ball.
- **Repair** pitch marks, spike marks and other damage on the green.
- **Flagstick:** you can leave it in or take it out when putting. Your choice.
- **Accidentally moving** your ball or marker on the green: no penalty; just replace it.

## Common situations

| Situation | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| You whiff (miss the ball completely) | It counts as a stroke |
| You hit the ball twice in one swing | Just one stroke, no penalty |
| Your ball hits you or your gear by accident | No penalty; play it as it lies |
| You accidentally move your ball (not on the green) | 1 stroke, replace it |
| Ball lands on a cart path or sprinkler head | Free relief (see [[penalties-and-relief]]) |
| You're not sure of the right ruling in stroke play | Play two balls, then ask the committee before you sign your card |

> [!tip] Casual vs competition golf
> In casual rounds, groups often agree to relax things: "preferred lies" on the fairway, or dropping near where a ball was lost to save walking back. That's fine for fun, but scores from those rounds shouldn't go in for handicap.
