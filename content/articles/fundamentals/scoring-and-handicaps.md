> [!key] Key points
> - **Stroke play:** count every shot. Lowest total wins.
> - **Stableford:** points per hole based on your net score. Highest points wins, and a blow-up hole only costs you that hole.
> - **Match play:** win holes, not strokes.
> - A **handicap** is the number of extra strokes you get, so players of different standards can compete fairly.

## The three main formats

| Format | How it's scored | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Stroke play** (medal) | Total strokes for the round. Every shot counts, so you must finish every hole | Club championships; tracking your real score |
| **Stableford** | Points per hole based on your net score | Most weekly club comps in Australia; casual rounds |
| **Match play** | Win, lose or halve each hole. The winner is whoever is ahead when there are more holes left than the gap | Head-to-head games with a mate |

### Stableford points
Your **net score** on a hole is your actual score minus any handicap strokes you get on that hole.

| Net score on the hole | Points |
| --- | --- |
| Net double bogey or worse | 0 |
| Net bogey | 1 |
| Net par | 2 |
| Net birdie | 3 |
| Net eagle | 4 |

Scoring **36 points** means you played exactly to your handicap. Once you can't score a point on a hole, pick up. It speeds up play.

### Match play terms
- **"2 up"**: you've won two more holes than your opponent.
- **"All square"**: level.
- **"3 & 2"**: won by 3 holes with 2 to play, so the match ended early.
- **Conceding**: you can give your opponent a putt (or a hole) at any time.

## How handicaps work

In Australia, handicaps are run by Golf Australia under the **World Handicap System**. In short:

1. Every score you enter for handicap is adjusted for how hard the course and tees were (the **Slope** and **Scratch Rating**).
2. Your **GA Handicap** (handicap index) is roughly the **average of your best 8 of your last 20** of these adjusted scores.
3. On the day, that's converted into a **Daily Handicap** for the course and tees you're playing. Your club's chart or the Golf Australia app does this for you.
4. Each hole has a **stroke index** (1–18) on the scorecard. If your Daily Handicap is 18, you get one stroke on every hole. If it's 22, you get two strokes on the four hardest holes (stroke index 1–4) and one on the rest.

> [!note] Bad holes don't wreck your handicap
> For handicap purposes, the most you can score on a hole is **net double bogey**. One disaster doesn't count for more than that.

## Getting a handicap

Join a club (or a social golf club affiliated with Golf Australia), then submit scores from rounds played under the rules. Your first handicap is calculated once you've lodged enough scores. Your club can tell you exactly how many and how to submit them.

> [!tip] Know your strokes
> Before each round, look at the stroke index column and circle the holes where you get a shot. A bogey on a hole where you get a stroke is a **net par**, which is 2 Stableford points.

> [!drill] Practise scoring
> On your next casual round, keep both a stroke total and Stableford points. You'll see how one double bogey hurts a stroke score badly but barely dents your Stableford.
