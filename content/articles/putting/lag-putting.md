> [!key] Key points
> - On long putts, **speed matters more than line**. A good line at the wrong pace still three-putts.
> - Control distance with **stroke length**, not by hitting harder.
> - Aim to leave every long putt **inside a 1-metre circle**.
> - **Look at the hole** during your practice strokes to let your eyes judge the distance.

## Why lag putting matters
Most three-putts come from the **first putt finishing too far away**, not from missed short putts. A first putt left 2 metres short or long is a real three-putt risk. Leave it inside a metre and two-putts become routine.

## How to control distance
1. **Same tempo, different length.** Keep your rhythm constant (see [[the-putting-stroke]]) and let the size of the stroke change the distance.
2. **Rehearse looking at the hole.** Make two practice strokes while looking at the hole, not the ball. Your eyes and hands are very good at matching distance when you let them.
3. **Strike the centre.** Off-centre hits come up short.
4. **Read the slope for speed.** Uphill putts need more; downhill and downgrain putts need much less.

## Where to aim
- **Uphill putts:** go for it. Finishing a little past leaves a simple uphill return.
- **Downhill putts:** aim to stop it at the hole or a little short. The return putt from below is easier.
- **Very long putts (15+ metres):** picture a 1-metre circle around the hole and just try to stop in it.

> [!mistake] Thinking about line on long putts
> From 15 metres, being 30 cm off line is fine; being 2 metres short is not. Pick a sensible line quickly, and put all your attention into the distance.

> [!drill] Ladder drill
> Put tees at 6, 9, 12 and 15 metres. Putt one ball to each. Then go back down. Every putt must finish past the previous one but short of the next tee. Restart if one doesn't.

> [!drill] Fringe drill
> From 10–15 metres, try to stop each putt **on the far fringe** without running off the green. It trains the feel of a putt that dies at a set distance.

> [!tip] Warm up with long putts
> Before your round, spend most of your putting warm-up on long and medium putts to find the speed of the greens. It saves more shots than knocking in short ones.
