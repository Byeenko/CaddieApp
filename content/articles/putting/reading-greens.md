> [!key] Key points
> - Find the **fall line**: the direction water would run off the green.
> - **Speed changes the break.** Softer putts break more; firmer putts break less.
> - The ball breaks **most near the hole**, where it's slowest.
> - Miss on the **high side** (the "pro side"); a ball below the hole can't go in.

## Start with the big picture
As you walk onto the green, look at the overall shape:
- Where is the **highest point** and the **lowest point**?
- Where would **water drain off**? Greens are built to shed water, usually towards the front or one side.
- Is the hole on a **tier** or a slope?

## Find the fall line
The **fall line** is the straightest downhill line through the hole. A putt straight up or down the fall line doesn't break. A putt across it breaks the most.

- Stand **behind the ball** and look at the hole, then walk to the **low side** and look from there. Reading from below the hole shows slopes more clearly.
- **Feel it with your feet.** Standing across a slope, you'll feel more pressure on your downhill foot.

## Speed and break are linked

The same putt hit softer breaks more, so it needs more aim. Decide your **speed first**, then the line that suits it. Most good putters aim for the ball to finish around **40 cm past** the hole if it misses. That's firm enough to hold its line, soft enough to drop in from the side.

## Grain
On some grasses, especially couch (Bermuda) greens common in warmer climates, the grass blades lean one way. That's the **grain**:
- Putting **down-grain** (the grass looks shiny): faster.
- Putting **into the grain** (the grass looks dull and darker): slower.
- **Across the grain:** the ball drifts in the direction the grass is growing.

Grain often grows towards where water drains, or towards the afternoon sun. On bentgrass greens it matters much less.

> [!tip] Pro side
> When in doubt, play a little **more** break. A putt that misses above the hole still had a chance to fall in; one that misses below never did.

> [!mistake] Under-reading
> Most club golfers don't allow enough break, and then hit putts too firm to make up for it. Allow more break and roll it at the right speed.

> [!note] AimPoint
> Some players use **AimPoint**, a structured method that feels slope with the feet and uses fingers held at arm's length to pick an aim point. It's worth a lesson with an accredited instructor if you want a systematic method.

> [!drill] Read, then check
> On the practice green, read a breaking 3–4 metre putt and put a tee where you'd aim. Hit three putts at that tee with good speed. If they miss low, you under-read. Repeat from different spots until your reads match reality.
