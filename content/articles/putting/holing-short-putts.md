> [!key] Key points
> - Hit short putts **firmly**, aiming at the back of the cup, so the ball holds its line.
> - **Pick a precise target**, like a blade of grass or the edge of the cup, and commit to it.
> - Keep your **head and body still** until the ball has gone; listen for it to drop.
> - Use the **same routine** every time.

## How often should they go in?
Even tour players miss: they make close to all of their 1-metre putts, but only around half of their 2.5-metre putts. Club golfers make fewer. Knowing this takes some pressure off. Missing a 2-metre putt is normal; rushing it or being tentative isn't.

## Firm and straight
From inside about 1.5 metres, most putts can be hit **firmly enough to take out most of the break**: aim inside the edge of the cup and hit it to finish 30–40 cm past. A firm putt is less affected by spike marks and small slopes than a dying one.

## Pick a tiny target
"The hole" is too big a target. Choose a specific point, like the back of the cup or a blade of grass on the edge, and hit the ball over a spot 10–20 cm in front of it on your line.

## Stay still
On short putts, the most common fault is moving your head or shoulders early to see the result. That opens the face. Keep your eyes on the spot where the ball was until you hear it drop.

> [!mistake] Being tentative
> Steering a short putt at dying speed lets it wobble off line. Commit to a firm, smooth stroke.

> [!mistake] Changing your routine under pressure
> Taking longer, re-reading three times, or hurrying to get it over with. Use exactly the same routine as on the practice green (see [[pre-shot-routine]]).

> [!drill] Circle drill
> Place six balls in a circle 1 metre from the hole. Hole all six in a row; miss one and start again. Then move out to 1.5 metres. Tour players use variations of this drill to build confidence they can trust under pressure.

> [!drill] Gate and pressure
> Set a gate (two tees just wider than the ball) 15 cm in front of the ball on a 1.5-metre putt. Hole ten in a row through the gate. Add pressure: if you miss, you owe yourself ten more.

> [!tip] Look at the hole
> If you suffer from nerves on short putts, try putting while looking at the hole instead of the ball. Many golfers find it frees up the stroke. It's worth an experiment on the practice green.
