> [!key] Key points
> - **Rock the shoulders**; keep the wrists quiet.
> - **The face at impact** decides most of where the ball starts, far more than the path.
> - **Backswing and follow-through about the same length**, with a smooth, even tempo.
> - **Hold your finish** and keep your head still until the ball is well on its way.

## Face beats path
Research using putting analysis systems shows the **clubface angle** at impact decides most of a putt's starting direction, roughly 80% or more. Path matters much less. A slightly arcing path with a square face holes putts; a perfect path with an open face misses right.

## The stroke
1. **Shoulders do the work.** Rock them like a seesaw, with the lead shoulder going down in the backswing and up in the through-stroke. Arms and putter move as one unit.
2. **Quiet wrists.** The angle in your wrists at address should stay the same through the stroke.
3. **Path:** most putters naturally swing on a **slight arc**, a little inside going back, square at impact, a little inside after. Let it happen; don't force a dead-straight line.
4. **Length:** match backswing and follow-through. Longer putts get a longer stroke, not a harder hit.
5. **Tempo:** the same rhythm for every putt, whatever the length. Many good putters count "one" going back and "two" through.

## Strike the centre
Off-centre strikes lose speed. A putt struck a little towards the toe can come up well short on a long putt. Hitting the sweet spot consistently is a big part of distance control.

> [!mistake] Decelerating
> A long backswing followed by slowing down into the ball. The face twists and speed varies wildly. Shorten the backswing and let the putter swing *through* the ball.

> [!mistake] Peeking
> Looking up to watch the ball before it's gone moves your shoulders, which opens the face. On short putts, listen for the ball dropping instead of watching it.

> [!drill] Gate drill
> Push two tees into the green just wider than your putter head, about 15 cm in front of the ball, on your line. Putt through the gate without touching either tee. Narrow it as you improve. It trains both start line and a square face.

> [!drill] Coin strike
> Put a small coin on the green and "putt" it so it slides straight, with no flicking or tumbling. Only a centred strike with a square face does that.

> [!tip] Tempo with a metronome
> Set a metronome app to around 70–80 beats per minute. Start the backswing on one beat and strike on the next. Use the same beat for 1-metre and 10-metre putts; only the stroke length changes.
