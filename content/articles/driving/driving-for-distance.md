> [!key] Key points
> - Distance = **speed × centred strike × launch conditions**.
> - A **centred strike** is often worth more yards than swinging harder.
> - For most golfers, **higher launch with less spin** goes further.
> - Speed can be **trained**, but don't trade your balance for it.

## The three ingredients

| Ingredient | What it means | How to improve it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Club speed** | How fast the clubhead is moving at impact | Better sequencing, speed training, fitness |
| **Strike (smash factor)** | How efficiently speed passes to the ball. Ball speed ÷ club speed, around 1.45–1.50 when you hit the middle | Centred contact; the right tee height and setup |
| **Launch & spin** | The angle the ball takes off at, and how much backspin it has | Hitting up on the ball, tee height, the right shaft and loft |

As a rough guide, each extra 1 mph of club speed is worth about **2–3 yards** of carry, *if* you still hit the middle of the face.

## Hit the middle first

A strike just an inch towards the heel or toe can cost **10–20 yards** and adds curve. Before chasing speed, find out where you're hitting it. Spray foot powder or dry-shampoo on the face, hit five balls, and look at the marks.

| Strike | Typical result |
| --- | --- |
| Centre | Best ball speed, straightest |
| Toe | Lower ball speed, tends to draw or hook |
| Heel | Lower ball speed, tends to fade or slice |
| High on the face | Higher launch, less spin: often fine |
| Low on the face | Low, spinny, short |

## Launch higher, spin less
Most club golfers launch the driver too low with too much spin. Hitting slightly **up** on the ball fixes both. That's why the [[driver-setup]] has the ball forward and the spine tilted back. If you still launch too low, more loft (10.5° rather than 9°) often helps more than a stronger swing.

## Building speed
- **Sequence:** speed comes from the ground up: lower body, then chest, then arms, then club (see [[the-downswing]]). Most "extra effort" goes into the arms and shoulders, which actually slows the club.
- **Swing smooth, finish fast:** the club should be fastest *after* the ball, not at the top.
- **Speed training:** short sessions of maximum-speed swings (with or without a training stick), a few times a week, are proven to build speed over weeks. See [[strength-and-speed]].
- **Mobility:** a bigger, freer turn creates more speed. See [[mobility-for-golf]].

> [!mistake] Swinging out of your shoes
> Going flat-out usually means a worse strike and a poor sequence: you're quick early and slow at the ball. If you can't hold your finish for three seconds, you swung too hard to hit the middle.

> [!drill] 80% vs 100%
> Hit five drives at 80% effort and five at 100%. Compare carry distance and how many finished in play. Most golfers find the gap in distance is small, and the 80% swings find more fairways.

> [!tour] On tour
> Tour players average around 113–115 mph of club speed with the driver. Their bigger advantage over club golfers is consistency: they hit the centre of the face over and over, with a launch and spin that suits their speed.
